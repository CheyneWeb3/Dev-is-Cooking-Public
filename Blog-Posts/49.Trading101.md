# What Is a Market Order?

A **market order** is an instruction to buy or sell an asset immediately using the best prices currently available in the market.

In simple terms, you're telling the exchange:

> **“I want to make this trade now. Give me the best price currently available.”**

The important word is:

## **Now.**

With a market order, completing the trade is the priority.

Getting one exact price is not.

---

# Market Buy vs Market Sell

There are two basic sides to a market order.

## 🟢 Market Buy

A market buy tells the exchange:

**“Buy this asset for me now.”**

For example:

**Buy $500 of BTC**

The exchange searches the available market and fills your purchase using the cheapest available sell orders.

---

## 🔴 Market Sell

A market sell tells the exchange:

**“Sell this asset for me now.”**

For example:

**Sell 0.005 BTC**

The exchange fills the order using the highest available buyers.

The trade happens using whatever liquidity is available at that moment.

---

# The Price on Your Screen Is Not Necessarily Your Final Price

This is one of the first things every new trader should understand.

Imagine Bitcoin is displayed at:

## **$100,000**

You press **Market Buy**.

That does **not** guarantee that every dollar of your order will purchase Bitcoin at exactly $100,000.

Why?

Because markets contain different buyers and sellers offering different amounts at different prices.

An order book might look something like this:

| Sell Price | BTC Available |
|---|---:|
| $100,000 | 0.01 BTC |
| $100,010 | 0.03 BTC |
| $100,025 | 0.10 BTC |
| $100,050 | 0.50 BTC |

If you want more Bitcoin than is available at $100,000, your order starts consuming the next available prices.

You might buy:

- some at **$100,000**
- some at **$100,010**
- some at **$100,025**
- and possibly more at **$100,050**

The result is your:

## **Average Execution Price**

---

# A Simple Token Example

Let's use an easier example.

Imagine a token is showing a current price of approximately:

## **$1.00**

Available sellers are offering:

| Amount | Price |
|---:|---:|
| 100 tokens | $1.00 |
| 200 tokens | $1.01 |
| 500 tokens | $1.03 |

Now you submit a market order to buy:

## **500 tokens**

The trade could fill like this:

**100 × $1.00 = $100**

**200 × $1.01 = $202**

**200 × $1.03 = $206**

Total spent:

## **$508**

You bought 500 tokens, so your average purchase price becomes:

## **$1.016 per token**

The token was showing approximately **$1.00**, but you didn't receive all 500 tokens for $1.00 each.

Your order consumed the available liquidity above that price.

---

# This Is Where Slippage Comes In

You'll hear the word **slippage** constantly in crypto trading.

Slippage describes the difference between the price you expected and the price you ultimately received.

For example:

**Expected price:** $1.00

**Average execution:** $1.016

Your trade experienced some price slippage.

Slippage is usually much smaller in highly liquid markets.

It can become significantly larger in thin markets.

---

# Liquidity Changes Everything

This is why understanding **liquidity** is so important when trading tokens.

Liquidity refers to how much buying and selling capacity exists around the current market price.

A highly liquid market may have:

- lots of buyers
- lots of sellers
- large amounts available
- small gaps between prices
- relatively low price impact

A low-liquidity market can have:

- very few buyers
- very few sellers
- large price gaps
- small amounts available
- significant price movements from relatively small trades

This means the same $10,000 market order could have almost no noticeable impact in one market and cause a huge price movement in another.

---

# What About Decentralised Exchanges?

Crypto makes this topic even more interesting.

On many decentralised exchanges — or **DEXs** — there isn't a traditional order book containing buyers and sellers.

Instead, you're often trading against a:

## **Liquidity Pool**

Imagine a pool containing:

**TOKEN A + USDC**

Instead of another trader directly selling TOKEN A to you, your swap interacts with the assets sitting inside that pool.

When you buy TOKEN A:

- TOKEN A leaves the pool
- USDC enters the pool
- the ratio between the assets changes
- the pool's calculated trading price changes

The larger your trade is compared with the pool, the greater the potential effect on the price.

This is known as:

## **Price Impact**

---

# Price Impact and Slippage Aren't Exactly the Same Thing

These two terms are often mixed together.

### Price Impact

The amount **your own trade changes the market price** because of its size relative to available liquidity.

### Slippage

The difference between the price you expected and the price you actually receive.

They are related, but they describe different things.

We'll cover both in much greater detail elsewhere in the **Devis Cooking Crypto & Trading Education Series**.

# Market Order vs Limit Order

The easiest way to understand market orders is to compare them with the next major type of order:

## Market Order

**Priority:** Execute the trade now.

You're effectively saying:

> “Buy or sell at the best available price.”

## Limit Order

**Priority:** Only trade at a particular price.

You're saying:

> “Only execute this trade if I can get my chosen price or better.”

Suppose ETH is currently:

## **$4,000**

A **market buy** might execute immediately around the current available market prices.

But a **limit buy at $3,950** tells the exchange:

**Do not buy unless ETH becomes available at $3,950 or lower.**

The advantage?

You control the maximum price you're willing to pay.

The disadvantage?

ETH could rise to $4,100, $4,200 and beyond without ever reaching $3,950.

Your order may never execute.

---

# Speed vs Price Control

This gives us one of the fundamental trade-offs in trading:

### Market Order

**More certainty of execution.**

**Less certainty of price.**

### Limit Order

**More control over price.**

**Less certainty of execution.**

Neither is automatically better.

They are tools designed for different situations.

---

# When Can a Market Order Be Useful?

A market order can make sense when:

- you want to enter a trade quickly
- you want to exit quickly
- the market has deep liquidity
- your trade is small compared with available liquidity
- completing the trade matters more than receiving one exact price

For example, if a trader needs to exit a position quickly because market conditions have changed, waiting for a perfect limit price may not suit their strategy.

---

# When Should You Be Careful?

Extra caution is warranted when:

- trading a low-liquidity token
- trading a newly launched token
- markets are moving extremely quickly
- the bid/ask spread is large
- you're placing a large order
- you're using a small DEX liquidity pool

In these situations, blindly submitting a large market order can produce a much worse execution price than expected.

---

# Why Small Crypto Tokens Need Extra Attention

This is especially important in crypto.

A token might display:

## **Price: $1.00**

But that number alone tells you very little about how much liquidity exists at $1.00.

A tiny pool might only support a few hundred dollars of trading near that price.

If someone suddenly attempts to market buy $20,000 worth of the token, the trade could push dramatically through the pool's price curve.

This is why traders should never look at **price alone**.

You also need to consider:

**Price + Liquidity + Trade Size**

---

# Before You Hit Market Buy or Market Sell

Take a moment to check:

- current price
- available liquidity
- trading volume
- bid/ask spread
- estimated fees
- expected execution
- trade size
- estimated price impact

When swapping through a DEX, also check:

- expected token output
- minimum token output
- slippage tolerance
- route
- liquidity pools being used
- network/gas fees

A few seconds of checking can prevent an expensive mistake.

---

# The Key Lesson

A market order does **not** mean:

> “Give me this exact price.”

It means:

> **“Execute my trade now using the best available prices.”**

That's a major distinction.

Remember:

## ⚡ MARKET ORDER = TRADE NOW

## 🎯 LIMIT ORDER = TRADE AT MY PRICE

Once you understand that, you've learned one of the basic foundations of trading.
