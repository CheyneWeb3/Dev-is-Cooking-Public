# **Candlestick Charts for Complete Beginners**

You learned that a standard candle shows:

- Open
- High
- Low
- Close

And that a green candle usually means:

## **Close > Open**

Simple enough.

But this is where beginners often make the next mistake.

They see:

# 🟢 A GREEN CANDLE

and immediately think:

> **“The market is going up.”**

Not necessarily.

A green candle only tells you what happened during one specific period of time.

It does not automatically tell you the direction of the larger trend.

So let's cook it down.

---

# What Does a Green Candle Actually Mean?

A green candle generally means:

## **The price closed higher than it opened during that candle's timeframe.**

That's it.

Imagine a one-hour candle:

**Open:** $100  
**Close:** $105

Because the close is higher than the open:

## The candle is green.

But that single candle does not tell you what happened:

- yesterday
- last week
- over the previous 100 candles
- on a higher timeframe

The market may still be in a major downtrend.

---

# Green Candle Does Not Automatically Mean Uptrend

Imagine a token falls like this:

## $100 → $80 → $60 → $40

Then it bounces:

## $40 → $45

That final move produces a green candle.

But look at the larger picture.

The token is still down from:

## $100 to $45

That is a:

## 55% decline

from the starting point.

So yes:

## The latest candle is green.

But the broader market may still be:

# **Falling**

---

# The Candle Shows a Period — The Trend Shows Structure

This is one of the most important distinctions in chart reading.

A candle describes:

## **One period of price action.**

A trend describes:

## **The broader sequence of price movement.**

You need multiple candles and market structure to identify whether price is actually trending upward or downward.

---

# Example: Green Candle Inside a Downtrend

Imagine this sequence:

**Day 1:** $100 → $90 🔴  
**Day 2:** $90 → $80 🔴  
**Day 3:** $80 → $70 🔴  
**Day 4:** $70 → $75 🟢  
**Day 5:** $75 → $60 🔴

Day 4 was green.

But the market was still clearly moving lower overall.

That green candle was simply:

## A temporary upward move inside a larger decline.

---

# These Moves Are Often Called Bounces

When a falling market temporarily moves upward, traders may call it:

- a bounce
- relief rally
- retracement
- pullback against the downtrend

The exact term depends on context.

But the key idea is:

## Not every upward move becomes a new uptrend.

---

# Red Candles Can Appear Inside Uptrends Too

The opposite is equally important.

Imagine a market moves:

## $50 → $60 → $70 → $80

Then falls:

## $80 → $75

That creates a red candle.

Does that automatically mean the uptrend is over?

No.

The market could simply be experiencing:

## A pullback inside the larger uptrend.

---

# Example: Red Candle Inside an Uptrend

**Day 1:** $50 → $55 🟢  
**Day 2:** $55 → $60 🟢  
**Day 3:** $60 → $65 🟢  
**Day 4:** $65 → $62 🔴  
**Day 5:** $62 → $70 🟢

Day 4 is red.

But the broader structure remains strongly upward.

So:

# Green ≠ automatically bullish trend.

# Red ≠ automatically bearish trend.

---

# Timeframe Changes Everything

Suppose Bitcoin is showing:

## 🟢 Green on the 5-minute chart.

You switch to:

## 1-hour chart

and it is red.

Then:

## Daily chart

is green.

Then:

## Weekly chart

is red.

Which one is correct?

## All of them.

Each chart is describing a different period of time.

---

# Example of Conflicting Timeframes

Imagine Bitcoin:

### Over the last 5 minutes

$100,000 → $100,500

## 🟢 Green

### Over the last hour

$102,000 → $100,500

## 🔴 Red

### Over the day

$98,000 → $100,500

## 🟢 Green

### Over the week

$110,000 → $100,500

## 🔴 Red

Same Bitcoin.

Same current price.

Different timeframe.

Different candle colour.

---

# This Is Why “Bitcoin Is Green” Is Incomplete

A better question is:

## **Green on what timeframe?**

Someone could say:

> “Bitcoin is pumping!”

while looking at a:

## 1-minute chart.

Another person could look at the daily chart and see a major decline.

Neither necessarily has bad data.

They are simply looking at different slices of time.

---

# Zooming Out Changes Perspective

Beginners often get trapped by staring at the latest few candles.

Imagine you open a chart and see:

## 🟢 🟢 🟢

Three green candles.

It feels bullish.

Then you zoom out and see:

## 🔴 🔴 🔴 🔴 🔴 🔴 🔴 🔴 🟢 🟢 🟢

Now the picture is very different.

Those three green candles may just be a small rebound after a major sell-off.

This is why:

# **Zoom out before deciding what the trend is.**

---

# Price Change Matters More Than Colour Alone

Let's compare two green candles.

### Candle A

Open:

## $100

Close:

## $100.20

Gain:

## +0.2%

### Candle B

Open:

## $100

Close:

## $120

Gain:

## +20%

Both candles are green.

But they represent dramatically different moves.

Candle colour alone does not tell you:

## **How much price actually moved.**

---

# The Starting Price Matters Too

Imagine Token A rises:

## $1 → $1.10

That's:

## +10%

Token B rises:

## $1,000 → $1,010

That's:

## +1%

Both increased by:

## $10?

No.

Token A only rose $0.10 but had the much larger percentage move.

Percentage change is often more useful than raw price difference when comparing markets.

---

# A Green Candle Can Still Close Far Below Previous Prices

Imagine:

Yesterday's close:

## $100

Today's market crashes to:

## $70

Then during one hour:

**Open:** $70  
**Close:** $75

That hour is green.

But $75 is still:

## 25% below yesterday's $100 level.

Calling the overall situation “bullish” simply because one candle is green would ignore the wider context.

---

# Higher Highs and Higher Lows Matter More

A common way traders identify an uptrend is by looking for:

# **Higher Highs**

and:

# **Higher Lows**

For example:

Low:

## $50

High:

## $60

Next low:

## $55

Next high:

## $70

Next low:

## $62

Next high:

## $80

The market keeps pushing its:

## highs higher

and:

## lows higher.

This is stronger evidence of an uptrend than simply seeing several green candles.

---

# Lower Highs and Lower Lows Signal the Opposite

A downtrend often contains:

# **Lower Highs**

and:

# **Lower Lows**

Example:

High:

## $100

Low:

## $80

Next high:

## $90

Next low:

## $70

Next high:

## $82

Next low:

## $60

There may be plenty of green candles during those temporary recoveries.

But the larger market structure keeps moving downward.

---

# A Green Candle Can Form a Lower High

This is a perfect example of why colour is not enough.

Imagine a downtrend:

Previous high:

## $100

Market falls:

## $70

Then a green rally pushes price to:

## $85

That rally may look bullish.

But:

## $85 < $100

The market has created a:

# **Lower High**

If it then falls below $70, the larger downtrend remains intact.

---

# A Red Candle Can Form a Higher Low

The opposite can happen during an uptrend.

Previous low:

## $50

Market rallies:

## $80

Then pulls back with several red candles to:

## $65

Since:

## $65 > $50

the market may still be maintaining a:

# **Higher Low**

The red candles didn't automatically destroy the uptrend.

---

# Trend Is About Structure, Not Colour Count

Imagine:

## 7 green candles

followed by:

## 1 enormous red candle.

If that red candle erases the entire previous move, simply counting candles would be misleading.

For example:

Seven green candles each rise:

## +1%

Then one red candle falls:

## -15%

There were more green candles.

The market still ended lower.

---

# One Big Candle Can Outweigh Many Small Candles

Consider:

### Five green candles

Each:

## +1%

Approximate total movement:

around:

## +5%

Then:

### One red candle

## -10%

There are:

## 5 green vs 1 red

But price is still substantially lower after the red move.

Candle count alone is not trend analysis.

---

# Percentage Change Gives Better Context

Instead of asking:

> “How many green candles are there?”

consider asking:

## “How much has price actually moved?”

For example:

Token starts:

## $100

After several candles:

## $80

The market is down:

## 20%

Even if the latest two candles happen to be green.

---

# Volume Adds More Context

Imagine a token falls heavily on:

## massive trading volume

then produces a tiny green candle on:

## very low volume.

That green candle may not represent the same level of buying pressure as the previous selling.

This does not guarantee price will keep falling.

But it demonstrates why:

## Colour without volume gives an incomplete picture.

---

# Example

Sell-off:

## $100 → $70

Volume:

## $50 million

Small rebound:

## $70 → $72

Volume:

## $500,000

Both movements appear on the chart.

But market participation was dramatically different.

---

# Liquidity Matters Too

A green candle on a tiny low-liquidity token can be extremely misleading.

Imagine a pool contains very little liquidity.

One trader buys:

## $2,000

worth of tokens.

The price jumps:

## 30%

The chart prints a huge green candle.

Someone looking only at the chart might think:

> “Massive demand!”

But the entire move may have been caused by one relatively small transaction.

---

# Large Green Candle Does Not Automatically Mean Large Money Inflow

This is especially important in crypto.

Price movement depends on:

## liquidity

not just the amount of money traded.

A small amount of capital can cause a huge percentage move in a shallow market.

A much larger amount might barely move Bitcoin in a deep market.

So:

# **Candle size does not directly equal money entering the market.**

---

# Market Cap Does Not Equal Money In Either

This connects to another common crypto misunderstanding.

If a token's price rises and its market cap increases by:

## $10 million

that does not necessarily mean:

## $10 million of new money entered the token.

Market cap is generally calculated as:

## Price × Circulating Supply

A relatively small trade can establish a higher marginal price.

That new price can then change the calculated market cap across the entire supply.

---

# Green Candle After a Crash Can Be a Dead-Cat Bounce

You'll sometimes hear the term:

# **Dead-Cat Bounce**

It describes a temporary recovery after a major decline before the larger downtrend resumes.

The term is informal and not a precise technical signal.

For example:

## $100 → $40

then:

## $40 → $55

then:

## $55 → $25

The move from $40 to $55 produced plenty of green candles.

But the broader decline continued.

---

# Relief Rally

A less colourful term is:

# **Relief Rally**

After heavy selling, traders may:

- take profits on shorts
- buy the dip
- rebalance
- react to temporary good news

This buying can create a temporary upward move.

Again:

## A rally does not automatically mean the previous downtrend has ended.

---

# Green Candle After Support

Now let's look at the opposite side.

Suppose price repeatedly holds around:

## $50

Then a strong green candle appears.

That may be more meaningful if it is accompanied by:

- strong volume
- a higher low
- a break above previous resistance
- improving broader structure

The candle itself still isn't the signal.

It's the:

# **Candle + Context**

---

# Red Candle at Resistance

Likewise, a red candle appearing after price reaches a major resistance area might provide useful information.

But a single red candle does not guarantee the market will reverse.

You still need to consider:

- structure
- volume
- liquidity
- timeframe
- broader trend

---

# The Market Can Be Bullish on One Timeframe and Bearish on Another

This idea is important enough to repeat.

Imagine:

## Weekly Trend

Bullish.

## Daily Trend

Correcting downward.

## 1-Hour Trend

Bearish.

## 5-Minute Trend

Bouncing upward.

A trader can legitimately describe all four depending on their timeframe.

This is called:

# **Multi-Timeframe Analysis**

---

# Multi-Timeframe Analysis

A simple approach is to look at:

## Higher timeframe

to understand the larger trend.

Then:

## Lower timeframe

to understand the immediate price action.

For example:

### Daily chart

Shows long-term uptrend.

### 1-hour chart

Shows temporary pullback.

### 5-minute chart

Shows a small bounce inside that pullback.

Each layer provides different information.

---

# Higher Timeframes Often Carry More Context

A single one-minute candle contains:

## 1 minute

of trading.

A daily candle contains:

## an entire day.

This doesn't mean higher timeframes are always “better.”

But they generally describe a larger amount of market activity and can provide broader context.

---

# Short Timeframes Are Noisier

Very short-term charts can contain a lot of:

## **Noise**

Small random movements can look dramatic when zoomed in.

A:

## 0.3% movement

might look like a huge candle on a one-minute chart.

On a yearly chart, it may be almost invisible.

---

# Chart Scaling Can Make Moves Look More Dramatic

Charts automatically adjust their vertical scale.

This can exaggerate how dramatic a move appears visually.

Imagine a token moves:

## $100 → $101

If the chart only displays:

## $99.50 to $101.50

that 1% move might fill most of the screen.

Visually:

## Huge move.

Mathematically:

## Only 1%.

Always check the actual numbers.

---

# Don't Judge Movement by Candle Height Alone

Look at:

## percentage change

and:

## price scale.

A very tall candle doesn't necessarily represent a massive percentage move.

A very small-looking candle on a long-term chart could represent billions of dollars of market value.

---

# Green Candle Does Not Mean Buyers “Won Forever”

A green candle simply means buyers pushed the close above the open during that period.

The next candle begins a new period.

New buyers arrive.

New sellers arrive.

Conditions change.

Markets are constantly auctioning price.

There is no permanent winner from one candle.

---

# What About an All-Green Chart?

Suppose a new token launches and prints:

## 🟢 🟢 🟢 🟢 🟢

Should you automatically buy?

No.

You still need to understand:

- liquidity
- token supply
- holder concentration
- contract permissions
- valuation
- volume
- who controls liquidity
- whether early holders are selling

Price action is only part of the picture.

---

# New Token Charts Can Be Especially Misleading

A new crypto token might launch at:

## $0.01

then trade:

## $0.02

## $0.05

## $0.10

That chart looks incredible.

But perhaps the pool only contains:

## a few thousand dollars of liquidity.

A large holder attempting to exit could dramatically reverse the price.

Always look beyond the green candles.

---

# A Green Candle Can Be Caused by Short Covering

Not all buying represents new bullish conviction.

Traders who are short eventually need to close their positions.

Closing a short may require buying.

That buying can push price higher.

So a green candle could partly represent:

## **Short covering**

rather than a flood of new investors.

---

# A Short Squeeze Can Create Huge Green Candles

If many traders are short and price suddenly rises, shorts may:

- close voluntarily
- hit stop losses
- be liquidated

This creates additional buying pressure.

The result can be:

## enormous green candles.

But once the squeeze ends, the market may behave completely differently.

---

# Liquidations Can Distort Short-Term Candles

The same thing happens in the opposite direction.

Heavily leveraged longs can be liquidated during a decline.

Forced selling creates:

## large red candles.

These movements may become self-reinforcing for a period.

This is another reason short-term candle colour does not necessarily describe the longer-term trend.

---

# News Can Produce Temporary Green Candles

Suppose positive news hits.

Price jumps:

## +10%

Then traders examine the details and realise the news was less significant than expected.

Price falls back.

The green candle recorded the reaction.

It did not guarantee the reaction would last.

---

# Candles Show Reaction — Not Truth

This is a useful mindset.

The chart shows:

## how traders reacted to information.

It does not tell you whether that reaction was:

- rational
- irrational
- temporary
- permanent
- correct
- mistaken

Markets can overreact in both directions.

---

# A Candle Cannot Tell You Why Price Moved

A green candle might have been caused by:

- real buying demand
- a whale purchase
- short liquidations
- low liquidity
- news
- arbitrage
- market-maker activity
- one unusual transaction

The candle records the price result.

Not the cause.

---

# This Is Why Context Matters

Before interpreting a green candle, ask:

### What timeframe is it?

### Where is it inside the larger trend?

### Is it making a higher high?

### Is it making a lower high?

### What is the volume?

### How much liquidity exists?

### Did something trigger the move?

### Has the candle actually closed?

These questions tell you far more than:

> “It's green.”

---

# Example 1 — Actually Bullish Structure

Price sequence:

## $50 → $60

Pullback:

## $60 → $55

Next rally:

## $55 → $70

Pullback:

## $70 → $62

Next rally:

## $62 → $80

We have:

- higher highs
- higher lows

Now green candles are occurring inside:

## **Bullish market structure.**

That is much more meaningful.

---

# Example 2 — Green Candles Inside a Downtrend

Price sequence:

## $100 → $70

Bounce:

## $70 → $80

Fall:

## $80 → $50

Bounce:

## $50 → $60

Fall:

## $60 → $30

There are multiple green rallies.

But every major high and low continues moving downward.

That is:

## **Bearish structure.**

---

# Example 3 — Sideways Market

Price moves:

## $90 → $100 → $92 → $101 → $91 → $99

Lots of green and red candles.

But the market remains trapped roughly between:

## $90 and $101.

This is:

# **A Range**

The candle colours change constantly.

The larger market isn't clearly trending either way.

---

# Common Beginner Mistake: Buying the First Green Candle

After a major sell-off, a trader sees:

## one green candle

and thinks:

> “The bottom is in.”

They buy.

Price immediately continues falling.

A green candle alone does not confirm a bottom.

---

# Common Beginner Mistake: Selling the First Red Candle

The same trader sees one red candle during a strong uptrend and panics.

They sell.

The market then continues higher.

Again:

## One candle is not a trend.

---

# Common Beginner Mistake: Ignoring Higher Timeframes

Someone watches:

## 1-minute candles

all day and forgets that the daily trend is moving strongly in the opposite direction.

Lower-timeframe movements often occur inside much larger structures.

---

# Common Beginner Mistake: Not Checking Candle Close

A candle looks strongly green halfway through its timeframe.

The trader buys.

By candle close, price has reversed and the candle finishes red.

Remember:

# **An open candle is still changing.**

---

# Common Beginner Mistake: Focusing Only on Colour

Candles contain far more information than red or green.

Look at:

- body size
- wick size
- high
- low
- open
- close
- location in the trend

Colour is only one part.

---

# A Better Beginner Routine

When you see a huge green candle, don't immediately ask:

> “Should I buy?”

Instead ask:

## 1. What timeframe is this?

## 2. How large is the move in percentage terms?

## 3. What did price do before this?

## 4. Is the market making higher highs and higher lows?

## 5. What's the volume?

## 6. What's the liquidity?

## 7. Has the candle closed?

## 8. Is this a breakout or just another bounce?

That turns a reaction into analysis.

---

# Green Is Information — Not an Instruction

This is probably the simplest way to remember the entire lesson.

A green candle means:

## **Price closed higher than it opened for that specific period.**

It does not mean:

## BUY

It does not mean:

## BULL MARKET

It does not mean:

## GUARANTEED PROFIT

It is simply:

# **Information**

---

# The Key Lesson

Candlestick colour tells you something very specific.

### 🟢 GREEN CANDLE

**Close was above open for that timeframe.**

### 🔴 RED CANDLE

**Close was below open for that timeframe.**

But trend requires more information.

Look at:

- highs
- lows
- timeframe
- percentage movement
- volume
- liquidity
- broader market structure

Most importantly:

# **Green does not automatically mean the market is going up.**

A green candle can appear:

- inside a downtrend
- during a temporary bounce
- during short covering
- during a low-liquidity spike
- before another major decline

Likewise, a red candle can appear inside a powerful uptrend.

Don't trade the colour.

## Read the structure.
