# **Support**

# **Resistance**

But beginners often make them much more complicated than they need to be.

So let's cook it down.

---

# What Is Support?

## **Support**

is an area where price has previously found enough buying interest to slow, stop or reverse a decline.

In simple terms:

> **Price falls into an area and buyers begin stepping in.**

Imagine a token falls:

## $100 → $80

It bounces from $80.

Later it falls again:

## $95 → $81

and bounces again.

Then later:

## $90 → $79

and buyers appear again.

Traders may begin viewing the area around:

## **$80**

as support.

---

# Support Is Not a Magical Floor

This is important.

Support does not mean:

> “Price cannot go below this level.”

It means:

## **Price has previously reacted positively around this area.**

Support can hold.

Support can weaken.

Support can break completely.

---

# What Is Resistance?

## **Resistance**

is an area where price has previously encountered enough selling pressure to slow, stop or reverse an upward move.

In simple terms:

> **Price rises into an area and sellers begin stepping in.**

For example:

A token rises to:

## $120

and falls.

Later it reaches:

## $119

and falls again.

Then:

## $121

and sellers appear again.

Traders may begin viewing the area around:

## **$120**

as resistance.

---

# Resistance Is Not a Magical Ceiling

Just like support isn't an unbreakable floor, resistance isn't an unbreakable ceiling.

Resistance simply means:

## Price has previously struggled to move through that area.

If buying pressure becomes strong enough:

## Resistance can break.

---

# A Simple Range Example

Imagine a token repeatedly trades between:

## $80

and:

## $120

Price falls toward:

## $80

and buyers appear.

Price rises toward:

## $120

and sellers appear.

You could describe this as:

### Support

around:

## $80

### Resistance

around:

## $120

The market is effectively trading inside a:

# **Range**

---

# Why Do Support and Resistance Exist?

There is no physical barrier sitting at a price.

Instead, these areas can form because of trader behaviour.

People remember prices.

They place orders around previous levels.

They react to:

- previous highs
- previous lows
- entry prices
- losses
- break-even levels
- psychological numbers
- known liquidity areas

All of this can cause buying or selling activity to cluster around certain prices.

---

# Example: Previous Buyers

Imagine a token previously bounced strongly from:

## $50

Some traders who missed the move may think:

> “If it comes back to $50, I'll buy.”

The token eventually returns.

Those waiting buyers may enter.

That can help create support around the same area again.

---

# Example: Trapped Buyers

Now imagine many traders bought at:

## $100

Price crashes to:

## $70

Months later it recovers toward:

## $100

Some of those traders may think:

> “I'm finally back to break-even. Get me out.”

They sell.

That selling pressure can contribute to resistance around:

## $100

This is one way previous price history can influence future behaviour.

---

# Support and Resistance Are Usually Zones

One of the biggest beginner mistakes is drawing support like this:

## Exactly $80.000000

and assuming price must reverse at that precise number.

Real markets are rarely that clean.

Price might react around:

## $78

## $79

## $80

## $81

## $82

So instead of thinking:

# **Line**

it can be more useful to think:

# **Zone**

---

# Why Zones Make More Sense

Imagine previous lows occurred at:

## $49.20

## $50.10

## $49.70

## $50.80

Calling:

## exactly $50

the only support price ignores the way the market actually traded.

A more realistic support zone might be roughly:

## $49–$51

depending on the chart.

---

# The Same Applies to Resistance

Previous highs might occur around:

## $99

## $101

## $100.50

## $102

Instead of treating:

## $100

as a perfect wall, traders may consider the wider region around it as resistance.

---

# Previous Lows Can Create Support

One common way traders identify support is by looking at:

## Previous swing lows.

Suppose a market falls to:

## $60

then rallies strongly.

That low becomes visually important.

If the market later approaches $60 again, traders may watch to see whether buyers return.

---

# Previous Highs Can Create Resistance

Likewise, a previous high can become an area of interest.

Suppose price reaches:

## $100

then sells off heavily.

If the market later returns to $100, traders may watch for another reaction.

---

# What Is a Swing High?

A:

## **Swing High**

is a local high point where price rose, peaked, and then moved lower.

For example:

## $80 → $100 → $90

The $100 area is a swing high.

---

# What Is a Swing Low?

A:

## **Swing Low**

is a local low point where price fell, bottomed, and then moved higher.

Example:

## $100 → $80 → $95

The $80 area is a swing low.

Swing highs and lows are often used when identifying support and resistance.

---

# The More Times a Level Is Tested, the More Attention It Gets

Imagine price reaches:

## $100

once and falls.

That may be interesting.

But if price reaches roughly $100:

- once
- twice
- three times
- four times

without breaking through, traders begin noticing the level.

More market participants may start watching it.

That can make the area increasingly important psychologically.

---

# But Repeated Tests Can Also Weaken a Level

This is another useful idea.

Imagine resistance at:

## $100

Sellers stop the market the first time.

Then again.

Then again.

But buyers keep returning.

Each test may consume some available sell liquidity.

Eventually buyers may overwhelm sellers and price breaks above.

So repeated tests can mean:

## The level is important.

But they do not mean:

## The level will hold forever.

---

# Support Can Break

Imagine a token repeatedly holds:

## $50

Then sellers become much stronger.

Price moves:

## $52 → $50 → $48 → $45

Support failed.

That is called a:

# **Support Break**

or:

# **Breakdown**

---

# Resistance Can Break

Likewise:

Price repeatedly fails around:

## $100

Then strong buying arrives.

Price moves:

## $98 → $100 → $103 → $110

The market broke above resistance.

This is commonly called a:

# **Breakout**

---

# Breakout Does Not Mean Guaranteed Continuation

A beginner sees price break resistance and thinks:

> “It broke out. It has to keep going.”

No.

A breakout means:

## Price moved through the previous resistance area.

What happens afterwards is still uncertain.

Price could:

- continue higher
- retest the old level
- move sideways
- fall straight back below

---

# What Is a Fake Breakout?

A:

# **Fake Breakout**

or:

# **False Breakout**

happens when price moves through an important level but fails to maintain the move.

For example:

Resistance:

## $100

Price rises to:

## $103

Traders believe it has broken out.

Then price falls:

## $103 → $98 → $90

The breakout failed.

---

# Why Fake Breakouts Matter

Many traders place orders around obvious support and resistance levels.

Around resistance, you may have:

- breakout buy orders
- short stop losses
- liquidation levels

A brief push through resistance can trigger these orders.

If buying then disappears, price can reverse quickly.

---

# Fake Breakdown

The same can happen below support.

Support:

## $50

Price suddenly falls to:

## $48

Stops are triggered.

Sellers panic.

Then buyers step in and price jumps back to:

## $55

The market briefly broke support but failed to remain below it.

This can be described as a:

## **Fake Breakdown**

---

# Candle Closes Matter

Remember our candlestick lesson.

Suppose resistance is:

## $100

During a one-hour candle, price reaches:

## $103

but closes at:

## $98

Did resistance really break?

Some traders would say:

## No confirmed breakout yet.

Price wicked above the level but failed to close above it.

This is why traders often watch candle closes around major levels.

---

# Wick vs Close

Imagine:

### Candle High

## $105

### Resistance

## $100

### Candle Close

## $97

The wick says:

> Price traded above resistance.

The close says:

> Price could not maintain that level.

That distinction can matter.

---

# Resistance Can Become Support

One of the most common concepts in technical analysis is:

# **Role Reversal**

Suppose resistance exists around:

## $100

Price finally breaks above it:

## $100 → $110

Then later price pulls back toward:

## $100

If buyers now defend that area, the old resistance has become:

# **New Support**

---

# Why Can This Happen?

Several reasons.

Traders who missed the breakout may think:

> “If it comes back to $100, I'll buy.”

Short sellers who sold near $100 may need to buy back.

Existing holders may see the retest as confirmation.

These behaviours can create new demand around the former resistance.

---

# Support Can Become Resistance

The reverse can happen.

Suppose support exists around:

## $50

Price breaks down:

## $50 → $40

Later the market rallies back toward:

## $50

Previous buyers may use the recovery as a chance to exit.

Short sellers may enter.

The old support can become:

# **New Resistance**

---

# A Simple Role-Reversal Example

### Stage 1

Resistance:

## $100

### Stage 2

Price breaks above:

## $105

### Stage 3

Price pulls back:

## $101

### Stage 4

Buyers defend the area and price rallies:

## $120

The previous $100 resistance has now acted as support.

---

# Support and Resistance Are Not Always Horizontal

Beginners often imagine only flat horizontal lines.

But traders may also identify dynamic areas using things such as:

- trend lines
- moving averages
- channels

However, horizontal support and resistance are generally easier for beginners to understand.

We'll cover trend lines in Lesson #59.

---

# Psychological Price Levels

Round numbers often attract attention.

Examples:

## $1

## $10

## $100

## $1,000

## $100,000

Why?

Because humans like simple numbers.

People often place orders around them.

For example:

> “I'll sell Bitcoin at $100,000.”

or:

> “I'll buy more if it reaches $90,000.”

This can create clusters of trading activity around round numbers.

---

# Psychological Levels Are Not Guaranteed Levels

Just because:

## $100

is a round number does not mean it must become support or resistance.

It simply means:

## More traders may pay attention to it.

Actual price behaviour still determines whether the level matters.

---

# Support and Resistance Depend on Timeframe

This is critical.

A support level visible on:

## a 5-minute chart

may be relatively unimportant on:

## a weekly chart.

Likewise, a major weekly support zone may influence price for months or years.

Always ask:

# **Support on what timeframe?**

---

# Higher-Timeframe Levels Often Attract More Attention

Imagine Bitcoin has repeatedly reacted around the same area on:

## Weekly candles

for several years.

That level may attract far more market attention than a random high from:

## five minutes ago.

This doesn't mean higher-timeframe support cannot break.

It simply means more market history may be associated with it.

---

# Support on One Timeframe Can Be Noise on Another

Example:

### 1-Minute Chart

Support:

## $100.20

### Daily Chart

Major range:

## $90–$110

That one-minute level may matter to a scalper.

It may be irrelevant to someone holding for months.

---

# Support and Resistance Need Context

A level becomes more meaningful when combined with other information.

For example:

- previous high or low
- strong volume
- repeated reactions
- higher timeframe
- major psychological level
- trend structure

This is often called:

# **Confluence**

---

# What Is Confluence?

Confluence means multiple pieces of information point toward the same area.

For example:

## $100

might simultaneously be:

- previous resistance
- a major round number
- a previous weekly high
- an area of heavy trading volume

That gives traders more reasons to pay attention to the area.

It still does not guarantee a reaction.

---

# Support and Resistance Are Not Predictions

A support level does not say:

> “Price will definitely bounce here.”

It says:

> **“This is an area where price has previously reacted, so it may be worth watching.”**

Likewise, resistance does not mean:

> “Short immediately.”

It identifies an area of interest.

---

# Don't Buy Support Blindly

Imagine support is:

## $50

Price falls toward it.

A beginner automatically buys because:

> “Support always holds.”

Then bad news hits.

Price breaks:

## $50 → $40 → $30

Support was historical information.

Not a guarantee.

---

# Don't Short Resistance Blindly Either

Resistance:

## $100

A trader automatically shorts every time price approaches.

Eventually enormous buying pressure arrives.

Price breaks:

## $100 → $110 → $130

The short is caught on the wrong side of a breakout.

---

# Trend Matters

Support and resistance behave differently depending on the broader trend.

In a strong uptrend:

## Resistance may break repeatedly.

In a strong downtrend:

## Support may fail repeatedly.

This is why identifying the trend matters.

---

# Support in an Uptrend

Imagine:

## $50 → $70

pullback to:

## $60

then rally:

## $60 → $90

The $60 area may become support within the uptrend.

If the market later returns there, traders may watch for another higher low.

---

# Resistance in a Downtrend

Imagine:

## $100 → $70

bounce:

## $70 → $80

then fall:

## $80 → $50

The $80 area may become resistance because it formed a lower high.

This connects directly to market structure.

---

# Support and Resistance Can Help Define Risk

Suppose a trader buys near:

## $50 support

Their trade idea may be:

> “I expect this support area to hold.”

If price clearly breaks below that zone, the original trade idea may no longer make sense.

Support can therefore help identify:

## Where the trade thesis becomes invalid.

This can assist with stop-loss planning.

---

# Resistance Can Help With Profit Targets

Suppose a trader enters at:

## $60

and major resistance exists around:

## $80

They may decide to:

- take some profit before resistance
- tighten risk
- watch how price reacts

Again, resistance does not guarantee a reversal.

It simply gives the trader an area to plan around.

---

# Support and Resistance Help Create a Trade Plan

Instead of:

> “I'll buy and see what happens.”

a trader might define:

## Entry

near support.

## Stop

below the level where the trade idea becomes invalid.

## Target

near resistance.

Now the trade has structure.

---

# Example Trade Structure

Support zone:

## $48–$50

Resistance zone:

## $60–$62

Possible entry:

## $50

Possible invalidation:

## below $47

Possible target:

## near $60

This does not mean the trade is good.

It simply demonstrates how levels can help define:

- entry
- risk
- target

---

# Risk-to-Reward Still Matters

Suppose:

Entry:

## $50

Stop:

## $49

Target:

## $60

Potential risk:

## $1

Potential reward:

## $10

Looks attractive.

But now imagine the support zone is extremely weak and price frequently moves several dollars.

The stop may be unrealistic.

Numbers alone are not enough.

Market behaviour matters.

---

# Support Can Be Tested From Above

After resistance breaks:

## $100 → $110

price may return to:

## $100

This is known as a:

# **Retest**

Traders often watch whether the old resistance can now hold as support.

---

# Resistance Can Be Retested From Below

After support breaks:

## $50 → $40

price may recover toward:

## $50

Traders may watch whether the old support now rejects price as resistance.

---

# Retests Don't Always Happen

A beginner may wait forever for:

## “the perfect retest.”

Sometimes price breaks resistance and continues straight upward.

Sometimes it breaks and instantly fails.

Sometimes it retests several times.

Markets do not owe traders a clean textbook setup.

---

# Breakout Volume

Traders often examine volume during breakouts.

Suppose price breaks resistance with:

## unusually strong volume.

That can suggest substantial market participation.

If price barely moves through resistance with almost no activity, some traders may be more cautious.

Volume does not guarantee success.

But it adds context.

---

# Liquidity Around Support and Resistance

Support and resistance can also attract:

- stop-loss orders
- take-profit orders
- limit orders
- liquidation levels
- breakout entries

This means significant liquidity can cluster around obvious chart levels.

When price reaches these areas, movement can become faster.

---

# Stops Below Support

Imagine many traders buy at:

## $100

because support exists around:

## $95

They may place stops around:

## $94

## $93

## $92

If price breaks support, those stops can begin executing.

That can add further selling pressure.

---

# Stops Above Resistance

Likewise, traders who short around:

## $100 resistance

may place stops at:

## $102

## $103

## $105

If price breaks resistance, those stops can trigger buying.

That can help accelerate a breakout or short squeeze.

---

# Why Price Can Move Quickly After a Break

Before support breaks:

## buyers are defending.

After it breaks:

- buyers may disappear
- stops may trigger
- shorts may enter
- existing holders may sell

The balance of liquidity changes.

That can produce a rapid move.

---

# Fake Breakouts Can Trap Traders

Suppose resistance is:

## $100

Price moves to:

## $102

Breakout traders buy.

Short sellers exit.

Then price immediately falls back below:

## $100

Now the new buyers are trapped at higher prices.

If they begin selling, the reversal can accelerate.

---

# This Is Why Confirmation Matters

Some traders look for things such as:

- candle close beyond the level
- increased volume
- successful retest
- continuation structure

before treating a breakout as confirmed.

No confirmation method is perfect.

It simply reduces reliance on one brief price spike.

---

# Support and Resistance on Low-Liquidity Tokens

Be especially careful with small crypto markets.

A thin token may appear to have clear support and resistance.

But one large wallet can completely change the chart.

For example:

Resistance:

## $1

A whale buys aggressively.

Price jumps:

## $1 → $2

The resistance did not mean much against the size of the order.

---

# Low Liquidity Creates More False Signals

Thin markets can produce:

- extreme wicks
- false breakouts
- sudden breakdowns
- rapid recoveries

Technical levels may be less reliable when very little liquidity exists.

Always understand the market you're analysing.

---

# Different Exchanges Can Have Different Levels

Suppose TOKEN X trades on:

## Exchange A

and:

## Exchange B.

Exchange A may show resistance around:

## $10

while Exchange B has slightly different highs because its order book and liquidity are different.

Crypto markets are fragmented.

There isn't always one perfectly identical chart everywhere.

---

# DEX Pools Can Have Different Prices Too

TOKEN/USDC on:

## DEX Pool A

may trade slightly differently from:

## DEX Pool B.

Each pool has its own:

- reserves
- liquidity
- trades
- price history

Arbitrage often keeps prices relatively close, but individual candles and levels can still differ.

---

# Horizontal Levels Are Subjective

Two traders can look at the same chart and draw slightly different zones.

One might mark support:

## $49–$51

Another:

## $48–$52

That does not necessarily mean one is wrong.

Technical analysis contains interpretation.

This is another reason not to treat one exact line as absolute truth.

---

# Keep Your Chart Clean

Beginners often draw:

## 25 support lines

and:

## 30 resistance lines.

Eventually every price touches something.

That makes the analysis useless.

Focus on the levels that appear most significant.

---

# A Simple Beginner Method

Start by looking for:

## 1. Major recent highs

## 2. Major recent lows

## 3. Areas where price reacted multiple times

## 4. Higher-timeframe zones

Then see how price behaves when it returns.

You do not need dozens of lines.

---

# How to Draw a Support Zone

Imagine several lows around:

## $48.50

## $49

## $50

Instead of drawing one exact line, you might identify:

# **$48–$50 Support Zone**

The exact width depends on the chart and volatility.

---

# How to Draw a Resistance Zone

Imagine repeated highs around:

## $99

## $100

## $102

You might mark:

# **$99–$102 Resistance Zone**

This acknowledges that markets rarely turn at the exact same decimal every time.

---

# Use the Candle Bodies and Wicks

Some traders draw levels using:

- candle bodies
- wick extremes
- combinations of both

There is no single universal rule.

The goal is not to find the mathematically perfect line.

The goal is to identify:

## **Where price repeatedly reacted.**

---

# Support and Resistance Can Move Over Time

Old levels can lose importance.

Markets change.

A level that mattered:

## three years ago

may be less relevant today.

New market structure can develop around entirely different prices.

Charts are dynamic.

---

# Price Discovery

When an asset breaks above its previous all-time high, there may be no historical resistance above it.

The market enters:

# **Price Discovery**

Buyers and sellers begin establishing entirely new price levels.

This can make price movement especially volatile.

---

# All-Time High Resistance

An asset's previous:

## **All-Time High — ATH**

often attracts significant attention.

For example:

If the previous ATH was:

## $100

traders may watch closely when price returns there.

If it breaks above:

## There is no previous historical price action above that level.

---

# All-Time Low Support

Likewise, an:

## **All-Time Low — ATL**

can attract attention.

If price breaks below it, the market is entering new downside price discovery.

Historical support below may no longer exist.

---

# Support Is Not the Same as “Cheap”

A token falling into support does not mean:

## The token is fundamentally cheap.

Support is a chart concept.

Valuation is a different question.

A terrible project can have technical support.

A great project can break technical support.

Do not confuse chart structure with fundamental value.

---

# Resistance Is Not the Same as “Expensive”

Likewise, resistance does not mean the asset is fundamentally overpriced.

It simply identifies an area where selling pressure previously appeared.

Price can break resistance and continue much higher.

---

# Common Beginner Support and Resistance Mistakes

## 1. Treating Levels as Exact Numbers

Think in zones.

---

## 2. Assuming Support Must Hold

Every support level can break.

---

## 3. Assuming Resistance Must Reject Price

Every resistance level can break.

---

## 4. Drawing Too Many Lines

If everything is a level, nothing is useful.

---

## 5. Ignoring Timeframe

Five-minute support is not the same as weekly support.

---

## 6. Buying Support Without a Risk Plan

A support level is not a guarantee.

---

## 7. Shorting Resistance Automatically

Strong trends can break resistance repeatedly.

---

## 8. Ignoring Volume and Liquidity

Levels behave differently in deep and thin markets.

---

## 9. Calling Every Wick a Breakout

A brief move beyond a level may fail.

---

## 10. Forgetting Role Reversal

Old resistance can become support, and old support can become resistance.

---

# A Better Beginner Routine

When price approaches an important level, ask:

## 1. Is this support or resistance?

## 2. Is it a zone or am I treating it like an exact line?

## 3. How many times has price reacted here?

## 4. What timeframe is the level from?

## 5. Is the broader trend up, down or sideways?

## 6. What does volume look like?

## 7. Is liquidity strong or weak?

## 8. Did price close beyond the level?

## 9. Is there a retest?

## 10. Where is my trade invalidated if the level fails?

Now you're thinking in terms of structure rather than simply drawing lines.

---

# The Key Lesson

Support and resistance identify areas where buyers and sellers have previously changed the direction or speed of price.

Remember:

### 🟢 SUPPORT

**An area where buying has previously slowed or reversed a decline.**

### 🔴 RESISTANCE

**An area where selling has previously slowed or reversed an advance.**

### 💥 BREAKOUT

**Price moves through resistance.**

### 📉 BREAKDOWN

**Price moves through support.**

### 🔄 ROLE REVERSAL

**Old resistance can become support, and old support can become resistance.**

But most importantly:

# **Support and resistance are areas of interest — not guarantees.**

Don't assume price must bounce because it reached support.

Don't assume price must fall because it reached resistance.

Watch how the market actually behaves when it gets there.
