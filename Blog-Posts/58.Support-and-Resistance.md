# **Support**

---

# What Is Support?

## **Support**

is an area where price has previously found enough buying interest to slow, stop or reverse a decline.

In simple terms:

> **Price falls into an area and buyers begin stepping in.**

Imagine a token falls:

## $100 → $80

It bounces from $80.

Later it falls again:

## $95 → $81

and bounces again.

Then later:

## $90 → $79

and buyers appear again.

Traders may begin viewing the area around:

## **$80**

as support.

---

# Support Is Not a Magical Floor

This is important.

Support does not mean:

> “Price cannot go below this level.”

It means:

## **Price has previously reacted positively around this area.**

Support can hold.

Support can weaken.

Support can break completely.

---

# What Is Resistance?

## **Resistance**

is an area where price has previously encountered enough selling pressure to slow, stop or reverse an upward move.

In simple terms:

> **Price rises into an area and sellers begin stepping in.**

For example:

A token rises to:

## $120

and falls.

Later it reaches:

## $119

and falls again.

Then:

## $121

and sellers appear again.

Traders may begin viewing the area around:

## **$120**

as resistance.

---

# Resistance Is Not a Magical Ceiling

Just like support isn't an unbreakable floor, resistance isn't an unbreakable ceiling.

Resistance simply means:

## Price has previously struggled to move through that area.

If buying pressure becomes strong enough:

## Resistance can break.

---

# A Simple Range Example

Imagine a token repeatedly trades between:

## $80

and:

## $120

Price falls toward:

## $80

and buyers appear.

Price rises toward:

## $120

and sellers appear.

You could describe this as:

### Support

around:

## $80

### Resistance

around:

## $120

The market is effectively trading inside a:

# **Range**

---

# Why Do Support and Resistance Exist?

There is no physical barrier sitting at a price.

Instead, these areas can form because of trader behaviour.

People remember prices.

They place orders around previous levels.

They react to:

- previous highs
- previous lows
- entry prices
- losses
- break-even levels
- psychological numbers
- known liquidity areas

All of this can cause buying or selling activity to cluster around certain prices.

---

# Example: Previous Buyers

Imagine a token previously bounced strongly from:

## $50

Some traders who missed the move may think:

> “If it comes back to $50, I'll buy.”

The token eventually returns.

Those waiting buyers may enter.

That can help create support around the same area again.

---

# Example: Trapped Buyers

Now imagine many traders bought at:

## $100

Price crashes to:

## $70

Months later it recovers toward:

## $100

Some of those traders may think:

> “I'm finally back to break-even. Get me out.”

They sell.

That selling pressure can contribute to resistance around:

## $100

This is one way previous price history can influence future behaviour.

---

# Support and Resistance Are Usually Zones

One of the biggest beginner mistakes is drawing support like this:

## Exactly $80.000000

and assuming price must reverse at that precise number.

Real markets are rarely that clean.

Price might react around:

## $78

## $79

## $80

## $81

## $82

So instead of thinking:

# **Line**

it can be more useful to think:

# **Zone**

---

# Why Zones Make More Sense

Imagine previous lows occurred at:

## $49.20

## $50.10

## $49.70

## $50.80

Calling:

## exactly $50

the only support price ignores the way the market actually traded.

A more realistic support zone might be roughly:

## $49–$51

depending on the chart.

---

# The Same Applies to Resistance

Previous highs might occur around:

## $99

## $101

## $100.50

## $102

Instead of treating:

## $100

as a perfect wall, traders may consider the wider region around it as resistance.

---

# Previous Lows Can Create Support

One common way traders identify support is by looking at:

## Previous swing lows.

Suppose a market falls to:

## $60

then rallies strongly.

That low becomes visually important.

If the market later approaches $60 again, traders may watch to see whether buyers return.

---

# Previous Highs Can Create Resistance

Likewise, a previous high can become an area of interest.

Suppose price reaches:

## $100

then sells off heavily.

If the market later returns to $100, traders may watch for another reaction.

---

# What Is a Swing High?

A:

## **Swing High**

is a local high point where price rose, peaked, and then moved lower.

For example:

## $80 → $100 → $90

The $100 area is a swing high.

---

# What Is a Swing Low?

A:

## **Swing Low**

is a local low point where price fell, bottomed, and then moved higher.

Example:

## $100 → $80 → $95

The $80 area is a swing low.

Swing highs and lows are often used when identifying support and resistance.

---

# The More Times a Level Is Tested, the More Attention It Gets

Imagine price reaches:

## $100

once and falls.

That may be interesting.

But if price reaches roughly $100:

- once
- twice
- three times
- four times

without breaking through, traders begin noticing the level.

More market participants may start watching it.

That can make the area increasingly important psychologically.

---

# But Repeated Tests Can Also Weaken a Level

This is another useful idea.

Imagine resistance at:

## $100

Sellers stop the market the first time.

Then again.

Then again.

But buyers keep returning.

Each test may consume some available sell liquidity.

Eventually buyers may overwhelm sellers and price breaks above.

So repeated tests can mean:

## The level is important.

But they do not mean:

## The level will hold forever.

---

# Support Can Break

Imagine a token repeatedly holds:

## $50

Then sellers become much stronger.

Price moves:

## $52 → $50 → $48 → $45

Support failed.

That is called a:

# **Support Break**

or:

# **Breakdown**

---

# Resistance Can Break

Likewise:

Price repeatedly fails around:

## $100

Then strong buying arrives.

Price moves:

## $98 → $100 → $103 → $110

The market broke above resistance.

This is commonly called a:

# **Breakout**

---

# Breakout Does Not Mean Guaranteed Continuation

A beginner sees price break resistance and thinks:

> “It broke out. It has to keep going.”

No.

A breakout means:

## Price moved through the previous resistance area.

What happens afterwards is still uncertain.

Price could:

- continue higher
- retest the old level
- move sideways
- fall straight back below

---

# What Is a Fake Breakout?

A:

# **Fake Breakout**

or:

# **False Breakout**

happens when price moves through an important level but fails to maintain the move.

For example:

Resistance:

## $100

Price rises to:

## $103

Traders believe it has broken out.

Then price falls:

## $103 → $98 → $90

The breakout failed.

---

# Why Fake Breakouts Matter

Many traders place orders around obvious support and resistance levels.

Around resistance, you may have:

- breakout buy orders
- short stop losses
- liquidation levels

A brief push through resistance can trigger these orders.

If buying then disappears, price can reverse quickly.

---

# Fake Breakdown

The same can happen below support.

Support:

## $50

Price suddenly falls to:

## $48

Stops are triggered.

Sellers panic.

Then buyers step in and price jumps back to:

## $55

The market briefly broke support but failed to remain below it.

This can be described as a:

## **Fake Breakdown**

---

# Candle Closes Matter

Remember our candlestick lesson.

Suppose resistance is:

## $100

During a one-hour candle, price reaches:

## $103

but closes at:

## $98

Did resistance really break?

Some traders would say:

## No confirmed breakout yet.

Price wicked above the level but failed to close above it.

This is why traders often watch candle closes around major levels.

---

# Wick vs Close

Imagine:

### Candle High

## $105

### Resistance

## $100

### Candle Close

## $97

The wick says:

> Price traded above resistance.

The close says:

> Price could not maintain that level.

That distinction can matter.

---

# Resistance Can Become Support

One of the most common concepts in technical analysis is:

# **Role Reversal**

Suppose resistance exists around:

## $100

Price finally breaks above it:

## $100 → $110

Then later price pulls back toward:

## $100

If buyers now defend that area, the old resistance has become:

# **New Support**

---

# Why Can This Happen?

Several reasons.

Traders who missed the breakout may think:

> “If it comes back to $100, I'll buy.”

Short sellers who sold near $100 may need to buy back.

Existing holders may see the retest as confirmation.

These behaviours can create new demand around the former resistance.

---

# Support Can Become Resistance

The reverse can happen.

Suppose support exists around:

## $50

Price breaks down:

## $50 → $40

Later the market rallies back toward:

## $50

Previous buyers may use the recovery as a chance to exit.

Short sellers may enter.

The old support can become:

# **New Resistance**

---

# A Simple Role-Reversal Example

### Stage 1

Resistance:

## $100

### Stage 2

Price breaks above:

## $105

### Stage 3

Price pulls back:

## $101

### Stage 4

Buyers defend the area and price rallies:

## $120

The previous $100 resistance has now acted as support.

---

# Support and Resistance Are Not Always Horizontal

Beginners often imagine only flat horizontal lines.

But traders may also identify dynamic areas using things such as:

- trend lines
- moving averages
- channels

However, horizontal support and resistance are generally easier for beginners to understand.

We'll cover trend lines in Lesson #59.

# Psychological Price Levels

Round numbers often attract attention.

Examples:

## $1

## $10

## $100

## $1,000

## $100,000

Why?

Because humans like simple numbers.

People often place orders around them.

For example:

> “I'll sell Bitcoin at $100,000.”

or:

> “I'll buy more if it reaches $90,000.”

This can create clusters of trading activity around round numbers.

---

# Psychological Levels Are Not Guaranteed Levels

Just because:

## $100

is a round number does not mean it must become support or resistance.

It simply means:

## More traders may pay attention to it.

Actual price behaviour still determines whether the level matters.

---

# Support and Resistance Depend on Timeframe

This is critical.

A support level visible on:

## a 5-minute chart

may be relatively unimportant on:

## a weekly chart.

Likewise, a major weekly support zone may influence price for months or years.

Always ask:

# **Support on what timeframe?**

---

# Higher-Timeframe Levels Often Attract More Attention

Imagine Bitcoin has repeatedly reacted around the same area on:

## Weekly candles

for several years.

That level may attract far more market attention than a random high from:

## five minutes ago.

This doesn't mean higher-timeframe support cannot break.

It simply means more market history may be associated with it.

---

# Support on One Timeframe Can Be Noise on Another

Example:

### 1-Minute Chart

Support:

## $100.20

### Daily Chart

Major range:

## $90–$110

That one-minute level may matter to a scalper.

It may be irrelevant to someone holding for months.

---

# Support and Resistance Need Context

A level becomes more meaningful when combined with other information.

For example:

- previous high or low
- strong volume
- repeated reactions
- higher timeframe
- major psychological level
- trend structure

This is often called:

# **Confluence**

---

# What Is Confluence?

Confluence means multiple pieces of information point toward the same area.

For example:

## $100

might simultaneously be:

- previous resistance
- a major round number
- a previous weekly high
- an area of heavy trading volume

That gives traders more reasons to pay attention to the area.

It still does not guarantee a reaction.

---

# Support and Resistance Are Not Predictions

A support level does not say:

> “Price will definitely bounce here.”

It says:

> **“This is an area where price has previously reacted, so it may be worth watching.”**

Likewise, resistance does not mean:

> “Short immediately.”

It identifies an area of interest.

---

# Don't Buy Support Blindly

Imagine support is:

## $50

Price falls toward it.

A beginner automatically buys because:

> “Support always holds.”

Then bad news hits.

Price breaks:

## $50 → $40 → $30

Support was historical information.

Not a guarantee.

---

# Don't Short Resistance Blindly Either

Resistance:

## $100

A trader automatically shorts every time price approaches.

Eventually enormous buying pressure arrives.

Price breaks:

## $100 → $110 → $130

The short is caught on the wrong side of a breakout.

---

# Trend Matters

Support and resistance behave differently depending on the broader trend.

In a strong uptrend:

## Resistance may break repeatedly.

In a strong downtrend:

## Support may fail repeatedly.

This is why identifying the trend matters.

---

# Support in an Uptrend

Imagine:

## $50 → $70

pullback to:

## $60

then rally:

## $60 → $90

The $60 area may become support within the uptrend.

If the market later returns there, traders may watch for another higher low.

---

# Resistance in a Downtrend

Imagine:

## $100 → $70

bounce:

## $70 → $80

then fall:

## $80 → $50

The $80 area may become resistance because it formed a lower high.

This connects directly to market structure.

---

# Support and Resistance Can Help Define Risk

Suppose a trader buys near:

## $50 support

Their trade idea may be:

> “I expect this support area to hold.”

If price clearly breaks below that zone, the original trade idea may no longer make sense.

Support can therefore help identify:

## Where the trade thesis becomes invalid.

This can assist with stop-loss planning.

---

# Resistance Can Help With Profit Targets

Suppose a trader enters at:

## $60

and major resistance exists around:

## $80

They may decide to:

- take some profit before resistance
- tighten risk
- watch how price reacts

Again, resistance does not guarantee a reversal.

It simply gives the trader an area to plan around.

---

# Support and Resistance Help Create a Trade Plan

Instead of:

> “I'll buy and see what happens.”

a trader might define:

## Entry

near support.

## Stop

below the level where the trade idea becomes invalid.

## Target

near resistance.

Now the trade has structure.

---

# Example Trade Structure

Support zone:

## $48–$50

Resistance zone:

## $60–$62

Possible entry:

## $50

Possible invalidation:

## below $47

Possible target:

## near $60

This does not mean the trade is good.

It simply demonstrates how levels can help define:

- entry
- risk
- target

---

# Risk-to-Reward Still Matters

Suppose:

Entry:

## $50

Stop:

## $49

Target:

## $60

Potential risk:

## $1

Potential reward:

## $10

Looks attractive.

But now imagine the support zone is extremely weak and price frequently moves several dollars.

The stop may be unrealistic.

Numbers alone are not enough.

Market behaviour matters.

---
