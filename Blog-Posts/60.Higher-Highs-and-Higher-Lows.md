# **Higher Highs and Higher Lows**

---

# What Is a Higher High?

A:

## **Higher High**

happens when price creates a new swing high above the previous swing high.

Example:

Previous high:

## $100

Price pulls back.

Then rallies to:

## $120

Because:

## $120 > $100

the market has created a:

# **Higher High**

---

# What Is a Higher Low?

A:

## **Higher Low**

happens when price pulls back but stays above the previous major low.

Example:

Previous low:

## $50

Price rallies.

Then pulls back to:

## $70

Because:

## $70 > $50

the market has created a:

# **Higher Low**

---

# Put Them Together

A classic uptrend often looks like this:

## Higher High

followed by:

## Higher Low

followed by:

## Higher High

followed by:

## Higher Low

And so on.

For example:

Low:

## $50

High:

## $70

Next low:

## $60

Next high:

## $85

Next low:

## $72

Next high:

## $100

The market is stepping upward.

---

# A Simple Uptrend Sequence

You can think of it like stairs.

### Step 1

Low:

## $50

### Step 2

High:

## $70

### Step 3

Pullback:

## $60

### Step 4

New high:

## $85

### Step 5

Pullback:

## $72

### Step 6

New high:

## $100

The market keeps producing:

# **Higher Highs**

and:

# **Higher Lows**

That is a classic uptrend structure.

---

# Why Higher Lows Matter So Much

Beginners often focus only on the highs.

They see:

> “Price made a new high.”

But the pullback tells you just as much.

Imagine price reaches:

## $100

Then falls to:

## $90

Then rallies to:

## $120

That $90 pullback may be healthy.

Why?

Because buyers stepped in:

## above the previous low.

That suggests demand is appearing at progressively higher prices.

---

# Higher Lows Show Buyers Stepping In Earlier

Imagine:

First pullback:

## $50

Second pullback:

## $60

Third pullback:

## $70

Buyers are no longer waiting for:

## $50

They begin buying around:

## $60

then:

## $70

That rising demand helps create the upward structure.

---

# Higher Highs Show Buyers Can Push Beyond Previous Peaks

Suppose price previously topped at:

## $100

It pulls back.

Then buyers push price to:

## $115

That tells us the market managed to exceed its previous major high.

The old ceiling was broken.

That is evidence of continued upward pressure.

---

# You Need Both Sides of the Structure

One higher high alone does not always mean:

## Strong uptrend.

Imagine price spikes from:

## $100 → $120

then crashes to:

## $50

Yes, the market briefly made a higher high.

But it destroyed the low structure.

A healthy trend generally involves both:

# Higher highs

and:

# Higher lows.

---

# Pullbacks Are Normal

This is one of the biggest lessons beginners need to understand.

Markets do not usually move:

## Straight up.

Even strong uptrends contain:

- red candles
- corrections
- pullbacks
- profit taking

A pullback does not automatically mean:

## The trend is over.

---

# Example of a Healthy Pullback

Price moves:

## $50 → $80

Then pulls back:

## $80 → $65

A beginner may panic because price dropped:

## $15

But the previous major low was:

## $50

The new low at:

## $65

is still a:

# **Higher Low**

The market structure remains bullish.

---

# Red Candles Can Build a Higher Low

Remember Lesson #57:

## Green does not automatically mean uptrend.

Likewise:

## Red does not automatically mean downtrend.

A pullback may contain several red candles.

Example:

## $100 → $95 → $90 → $85

If the previous major low was:

## $70

then $85 may still form a:

## Higher Low.

The red candles happened inside an uptrend.

---

# Market Structure Matters More Than Candle Colour

Compare these two charts.

### Chart A

Ten green candles.

Then price breaks below the previous major low.

### Chart B

Several red candles.

But price keeps forming higher lows and then makes new highs.

Chart B may actually have the healthier bullish structure.

This is why traders focus on:

## Highs and lows

not just:

## Red and green.

---

# What Is a Swing High?

A:

## **Swing High**

is a local peak where price rises, turns, and moves lower.

Example:

## $80 → $100 → $90

The:

## $100

area is a swing high.

---

# What Is a Swing Low?

A:

## **Swing Low**

is a local bottom where price falls, turns, and moves higher.

Example:

## $100 → $80 → $95

The:

## $80

area is a swing low.

Higher highs and higher lows are built from these swing points.

---

# Not Every Tiny Move Is a Meaningful Swing

On a noisy chart, price might move:

## $100 → $101 → $100.50 → $102 → $101.80

If you label every tiny movement as a swing high or low, the chart becomes useless.

Focus on:

## Significant turning points

for the timeframe you're analysing.

---

# Timeframe Matters

A higher high on the:

## 1-minute chart

may be completely irrelevant to the:

## Daily trend.

A trader can see:

### 5-minute chart

Higher highs and higher lows.

### Daily chart

Still in a major downtrend.

Both can be true.

---

# Example of Different Timeframes

Suppose Bitcoin has fallen:

## $120,000 → $90,000

over several weeks.

Today it moves:

## $90,000 → $95,000 → $92,000 → $98,000

On a short timeframe, that may create:

## Higher highs and higher lows.

But the broader weekly structure may still be bearish.

Always know which timeframe your structure belongs to.

---

# Higher-Timeframe Structure Usually Gives Broader Context

A daily sequence of higher highs and higher lows represents much more price history than:

## three 1-minute candles.

That does not make short-term structure useless.

It simply means:

## Different structures matter to different traders.

---

# What Is Trend Continuation?

Suppose the market already has an uptrend.

Price forms:

## Higher High

then:

## Higher Low

then pushes above the previous high again.

That new breakout can be considered:

# **Trend Continuation**

The market is continuing the existing upward structure.

---

# Example

High:

## $100

Pullback low:

## $90

New high:

## $120

Pullback low:

## $105

New high:

## $140

Each pullback holds above the previous major low.

Each rally exceeds the previous high.

That is clean bullish structure.

---

# What Happens When Price Fails to Make a Higher High?

Suppose:

Previous high:

## $100

Price pulls back.

Then rallies only to:

## $95

and falls again.

The market failed to create a higher high.

Instead, it created a:

## Lower High

That can be the first warning that momentum is weakening.

---

# A Failed Higher High Does Not Automatically Mean Reversal

The market could simply:

- consolidate
- move sideways
- build a range
- attempt another breakout later

One failed high is not enough to declare a new downtrend.

Again:

## Context matters.

---

# What Happens When a Higher Low Fails?

This is more important.

Suppose:

Previous major low:

## $80

Next higher low:

## $90

Market rallies.

Then price falls and breaks below:

## $90

This can weaken the bullish structure.

If price then breaks below the earlier major low at:

## $80

the uptrend structure is even more seriously damaged.

---

# Structure Break

When price breaks an important previous swing point, traders may describe it as a:

# **Break of Structure**

or:

# **BOS**

For example:

Uptrend has lows:

## $50 → $60 → $70

Price then falls below:

## $70

This may be the first sign of weakness.

If it continues below:

## $60

the broader structure becomes increasingly bearish.

---

# Break of Structure Does Not Mean Guaranteed Reversal

This term is sometimes treated too dramatically.

A structure break tells you:

## Something changed.

It does not guarantee:

## The market will now collapse.

Price could:

- recover
- fake out
- form a range
- create a new trend

Use it as information.

Not prophecy.

---

# Change of Character

You may also hear:

# **Change of Character**

or:

# **CHOCH**

Different traders use this term slightly differently.

Broadly, it refers to early evidence that the market's previous behaviour may be changing.

For example:

A strong uptrend stops making higher highs and begins breaking important higher lows.

That may indicate a possible shift in character.

---

# Keep It Simple as a Beginner

You do not need complicated terminology to understand the basic idea.

Ask:

## Is price still making higher highs?

## Is price still making higher lows?

If yes:

## The uptrend may still be intact.

If not:

## Something may be changing.

Simple.

---

# Higher Highs Can Break Resistance

Remember Lesson #58.

If previous resistance sits at:

## $100

and price rallies to:

## $110

the market has:

- broken resistance
- created a higher high

These ideas connect.

Technical analysis is really several ways of describing the same price behaviour.

---

# Higher Lows Can Form Support

Suppose the market pulls back:

## $100 → $90

then rallies.

Later it returns to:

## $92

and bounces again.

That higher-low area may begin acting as:

## Support.

Again, market structure and support/resistance are connected.

---

# Trend Lines Connect Higher Lows

Remember Lesson #59.

In an uptrend, a rising trend line is often drawn underneath:

# **Higher Lows**

So:

- higher lows create the structure
- the trend line helps visualise it

The line is not the trend itself.

---

# Example of Trend Line + Market Structure

Lows:

## $50

## $60

## $72

Highs:

## $70

## $85

## $105

A rising trend line connects the lows.

The higher highs confirm that price is also pushing upward.

The line and structure support the same interpretation.

---

# Strong Uptrend vs Weak Uptrend

Not all uptrends look the same.

### Strong Uptrend

Price rises sharply.

Pullbacks are shallow.

Higher highs form quickly.

### Weak Uptrend

Price rises slowly.

Pullbacks are deeper.

New highs are only slightly higher.

Both can still technically be uptrends.

But their momentum differs.

---

# Shallow Pullbacks

Imagine:

High:

## $100

Pullback:

## $95

Next high:

## $120

Pullback:

## $115

Buyers are stepping in very quickly.

This can indicate strong demand.

---

# Deep Pullbacks

Imagine:

High:

## $100

Pullback:

## $75

Next high:

## $110

Pullback:

## $80

The market is still creating higher lows.

But the pullbacks are much deeper.

That may indicate a less stable trend.

---

# Momentum Can Change Before Structure Breaks

Suppose each new high becomes smaller.

Example:

Highs:

## $100

## $120

## $130

## $135

The market is still making higher highs.

But the distance between them is shrinking.

Meanwhile pullbacks become deeper.

The trend may still be bullish, but momentum could be weakening.

---

# Price Can Consolidate During an Uptrend

Imagine:

## $50 → $100

Then price trades sideways between:

## $90 and $100

for several weeks.

There may be no clear new higher high or higher low during that period.

The market is:

# **Consolidating**

This does not automatically mean the uptrend ended.

It may simply be pausing.

---

# What Is Consolidation?

Consolidation occurs when price trades within a relatively defined range rather than trending strongly.

For example:

Support:

## $90

Resistance:

## $100

Price repeatedly moves between them.

Eventually it may break:

- upward
- downward

Until then, the market is range-bound.

---

# New Higher High After Consolidation

Suppose an uptrend pauses between:

## $90–$100

Then price breaks:

## $100 → $115

A new higher high forms.

That can represent continuation of the larger uptrend.

---

# Breakdown After Consolidation

Alternatively:

Price breaks:

## $90 → $80

Now the previous bullish structure may be weakening.

The direction of the range breakout matters.

---

# Higher Highs in Low-Liquidity Markets

Be careful with small tokens.

A low-liquidity token can produce a huge higher high because:

## one large buy moved the pool.

The chart might look strongly bullish.

But the move may not represent broad demand.

Always consider:

- liquidity
- volume
- number of traders
- holder concentration

---

# One Trade Can Create a Fake Higher High

Imagine previous high:

## $1

A whale market buys.

Price briefly spikes to:

## $1.50

Then immediately falls back to:

## $0.80

Technically, the chart printed a higher high.

But the move was not sustained.

That wick alone may not represent healthy market structure.

---

# Candle Closes Matter

Some traders pay more attention to:

## Closing prices

than brief wick extremes.

For example:

Previous high:

## $100

Price wicks to:

## $105

but closes at:

## $95

Did the market truly break the high?

Different traders may interpret it differently.

This is why structure is not always perfectly objective.

---

# Wick High vs Confirmed High

A brief wick above resistance may simply be:

- liquidity grab
- temporary spike
- failed breakout

A strong candle close above the previous high may carry more weight for some strategies.

There is no single universal rule.

---

# Higher High Does Not Mean “Buy Now”

This is another beginner trap.

Price makes a new high.

The trader thinks:

> “Uptrend confirmed. Buy.”

Then price immediately pulls back.

Market structure describes what the market is doing.

It does not automatically give you an entry.

You still need:

- risk management
- entry plan
- stop
- position sizing

---

# Buying the Top of Every Higher High Can Be Dangerous

Imagine:

## $50 → $70

You buy at:

## $70

Price pulls back to:

## $60

You panic.

Then price rallies to:

## $85

You buy again at $85.

It pulls back to:

## $72

The uptrend was perfectly healthy.

But the trader kept entering after extended moves and panicking during normal pullbacks.

Understanding structure can help avoid this.

---

# Higher Lows Can Offer Better Risk Context

Some traders prefer to watch pullbacks because the nearby higher low may provide a clearer invalidation point.

Example:

Trend:

## $50 → $70 → $60 → $85

Price pulls back toward:

## $72

If the trade idea is based on continued bullish structure, the trader can assess risk relative to the previous swing lows.

This does not automatically make it a good entry.

It simply provides clearer structure.

---

# Stop Placement Around Higher Lows

Suppose the latest significant higher low is:

## $70

A trader may decide:

> “If price breaks clearly below this area, my bullish thesis weakens.”

That can help define risk.

But placing a stop exactly one cent below the low can be vulnerable to:

- wicks
- volatility
- stop clusters

Use market context.

---

# Don't Keep Redefining the Higher Low to Avoid Being Wrong

A common emotional mistake:

Original important low:

## $70

Price breaks to:

## $65

Trader says:

> “Actually $65 is the real higher low.”

Price breaks to:

## $60

> “Actually $60.”

Eventually the original trend structure is clearly gone.

Analysis should adapt to the market.

Not be rewritten just to justify staying in a losing trade.

---

# Volume Can Add Context

Imagine price creates a higher high on:

## very strong volume.

That can indicate significant participation.

Now imagine a tiny wick creates a higher high on almost no volume.

Those two situations are not identical.

Volume adds another layer of information.

---

# Strong Breakout Example

Previous high:

## $100

Price breaks to:

## $115

with:

- strong volume
- large candle body
- close above resistance

Then price pulls back and holds:

## $105

This looks structurally stronger than a brief wick above $100 followed by an immediate collapse.

---

# Higher Highs Can Be Driven by Short Squeezes

Remember:

Not all buying is new bullish investment.

A breakout above a previous high may trigger:

- short stops
- short liquidations
- breakout buys

This can accelerate the move upward.

The structure still matters.

But understanding what may be driving it adds context.

---

# What Happens After a Higher High?

Three broad possibilities:

## 1. Price continues upward

Strong momentum.

## 2. Price pulls back and creates a higher low

Healthy trend continuation.

## 3. Price collapses below previous structure

Potential trend failure.

The higher high itself is only one stage of the process.

---

# How Do You Know When an Uptrend Ends?

There is no single perfect signal.

But warning signs may include:

- failure to make new higher highs
- lower highs forming
- important higher lows breaking
- support failing
- rising trend line breaking
- selling volume increasing

The more of these occur together, the stronger the evidence that conditions are changing.

---

# Example of Trend Weakening

Highs:

## $100

## $120

## $125

Then:

## $123

Lows:

## $80

## $95

## $110

Then price breaks:

## $110 → $100

The market:

- failed to create another higher high
- broke the previous higher low

The bullish structure has weakened significantly.

---

# Example of Full Structure Shift

Bullish structure:

High:

## $120

Low:

## $100

Price fails to exceed $120 and only reaches:

## $110

That's a:

## Lower High.

Then price falls below:

## $100

to:

## $90

That's a:

## Lower Low.

The market has transitioned from:

## Higher highs + higher lows

toward:

## Lower highs + lower lows.

That is a much stronger sign of trend change.

---

# Structure Can Change Gradually

Markets do not always flip instantly from:

## Uptrend

to:

## Downtrend.

They may go:

## Uptrend → Range → Downtrend

or:

## Uptrend → Consolidation → New Uptrend

There can be long periods where structure is unclear.

You do not always need to label every market immediately.

---

# “I Don't Know” Is a Valid Market View

If price is making:

- mixed highs
- mixed lows
- sideways movement

the correct conclusion might simply be:

## No clear trend.

Forcing bullish or bearish labels onto unclear markets can lead to bad trades.

---

# Higher Highs and Higher Lows on a DEX

The concept works the same way.

A DEX token chart may show:

- higher pool prices
- pullbacks
- new highs
- new higher lows

But remember that DEX prices depend heavily on:

## Liquidity pool reserves.

Low liquidity can create exaggerated structure.

---

# Arbitrage Can Affect DEX Structure

If the same token trades across multiple pools, arbitrage traders may push prices back toward one another.

A sudden higher high on one pool may disappear quickly if that market becomes mispriced relative to others.

That is another reason to know:

## Which market your chart represents.

---

# Market Structure Is Not Fundamental Value

A token can have beautiful higher highs and higher lows while still being:

- overvalued
- risky
- malicious
- illiquid

Technical structure only describes price behaviour.

It does not tell you whether the project is good.

---

# Common Beginner Higher-High / Higher-Low Mistakes

## 1. Labeling Every Tiny Move

Focus on meaningful swing points.

---

## 2. Ignoring Timeframe

A one-minute uptrend can exist inside a weekly downtrend.

---

## 3. Treating One Higher High as Proof

You also need to watch the pullback structure.

---

## 4. Panicking During Normal Pullbacks

Red candles do not automatically destroy an uptrend.

---

## 5. Ignoring a Broken Higher Low

If major structure fails, update the analysis.

---

## 6. Buying Every New High

Trend identification is not the same as entry timing.

---

## 7. Ignoring Liquidity

Thin markets can create misleading highs and lows.

---

## 8. Forcing Bullish Structure

Do not relabel failed lows just to support the trade you already want.

---

# A Better Beginner Routine

When looking for an uptrend, ask:

## 1. Where is the previous major swing high?

## 2. Did price create a higher high?

## 3. Where is the previous swing low?

## 4. Did the pullback create a higher low?

## 5. What timeframe am I analysing?

## 6. Is volume supporting the move?

## 7. Is there enough liquidity?

## 8. Did price close above previous resistance or only wick through it?

## 9. Is the trend accelerating or weakening?

## 10. What would invalidate the bullish structure?

That gives you a much clearer picture than:

> “There are lots of green candles.”

## $70 → $85 → $100

# The Key Lesson

Higher highs and higher lows are one of the simplest ways to identify an uptrend.

Remember:

### ⬆️ HIGHER HIGH

**A new significant high above the previous high.**

### 📈 HIGHER LOW

**A pullback that remains above the previous important low.**

### 🔄 PULLBACK

**A temporary move against the trend that does not necessarily end it.**

### 💥 STRUCTURE BREAK

**Price breaks an important swing level and signals that the previous pattern may be changing.**

Most importantly:

# **Uptrends are not made of green candles. They are made of structure.**

A healthy uptrend can contain plenty of:

- red candles
- corrections
- pullbacks

What matters is whether the market continues producing:

# **Higher Highs and Higher Lows**

Once that structure begins failing, pay attention.

The market may be telling you something has changed.
