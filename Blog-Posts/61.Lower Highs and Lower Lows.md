Welcome back to the **Devis Cooking Crypto & Trading Education Series**, where we break down crypto, blockchain and trading concepts one ingredient at a time.

# **Higher Highs and Higher Lows**

That is the classic structure of an:

# 📈 Uptrend

Now we're going to flip the chart around.

When a market is trending downward, it commonly creates:

# **Lower Highs**

and:

# **Lower Lows**

This is one of the simplest ways to recognise bearish market structure.

And just like an uptrend can contain plenty of red candles, a downtrend can contain plenty of green candles.

The key is not simply candle colour.

The key is:

# **Structure**

So let's cook it down.

---

# What Is a Lower High?

A:

## **Lower High**

happens when price rallies but fails to reach the previous major high.

For example:

Previous high:

## $100

Price falls.

Then rallies to:

## $90

Because:

## $90 < $100

the market has created a:

# **Lower High**

---

# What Is a Lower Low?

A:

## **Lower Low**

happens when price falls below the previous major low.

Example:

Previous low:

## $70

Price rallies.

Then falls to:

## $60

Because:

## $60 < $70

the market has created a:

# **Lower Low**

---

# Put Them Together

A classic downtrend often looks like:

## Lower High

followed by:

## Lower Low

followed by:

## Lower High

followed by:

## Lower Low

For example:

High:

## $100

Low:

## $80

Next high:

## $90

Next low:

## $70

Next high:

## $82

Next low:

## $60

The market is stepping downward.

---

# Think of It Like Stairs Going Down

A bearish market can be visualised as a staircase.

### Step 1

High:

## $100

### Step 2

Low:

## $80

### Step 3

Rally:

## $90

### Step 4

New low:

## $70

### Step 5

Rally:

## $82

### Step 6

New low:

## $60

Every rally fails below the previous major high.

Every decline pushes below the previous major low.

That is classic:

# **Bearish Market Structure**

---

# Lower Highs Show Sellers Stepping In Earlier

Imagine the first major high is:

## $100

After a decline, price recovers only to:

## $90

Then later only to:

## $80

Sellers are no longer waiting for price to reach $100.

They are becoming active at:

## lower and lower prices.

This can indicate persistent selling pressure.

---

# Lower Lows Show Buyers Are Failing to Hold Previous Support

Suppose the market previously bounced from:

## $70

Later it falls below that level to:

## $60

The previous support failed.

Price has created a:

## Lower Low.

This tells us sellers managed to push the market into new lower territory.

---

# You Need Both Sides of the Structure

One lower low alone does not necessarily prove a strong downtrend.

Imagine price briefly falls:

## $100 → $90

then immediately rallies to:

## $130

Yes, a lower price was printed.

But the market then completely changed direction.

A cleaner bearish trend usually shows both:

# Lower Highs

and:

# Lower Lows

forming repeatedly.

---

# Green Candles Are Normal Inside a Downtrend

This is one of the biggest beginner mistakes.

A trader sees:

## 🟢 🟢 🟢

and thinks:

> “The downtrend is over.”

Not necessarily.

A bearish market still needs rallies.

Price rarely falls in a perfectly straight line.

Those rallies can create:

- green candles
- sharp bounces
- short squeezes
- relief rallies

while the broader structure remains bearish.

---

# Example of a Rally Inside a Downtrend

Price falls:

## $100 → $70

Then rallies:

## $70 → $85

That rally may look strong.

But the previous major high was:

## $100

Since:

## $85 < $100

the market has formed a:

# **Lower High**

If price then falls below $70:

## $70 → $60

it creates another:

# **Lower Low**

The downtrend continues.

---

# A Green Rally Can Still Be Bearish Structure

Imagine:

Previous high:

## $120

Market falls to:

## $80

Then several green candles push price back to:

## $105

Someone zoomed in might think:

> “Strong bullish move.”

But the larger structure says:

## $105 < $120

So the rally has still only created a:

# Lower High.

That is why timeframe and context matter.

---

# What Is a Bear Market Rally?

A:

## **Bear Market Rally**

is a substantial upward move occurring inside a larger bearish market.

These rallies can be powerful.

Sometimes they rise:

- 10%
- 20%
- 30%
- or more

before the larger downtrend resumes.

A strong bounce does not automatically mean a new bull market has started.

---

# Relief Rally

Another common term is:

# **Relief Rally**

After heavy selling, the market may bounce because:

- sellers take profit
- short positions close
- bargain buyers enter
- panic temporarily eases

This can create a sharp upward move.

But the broader trend may remain bearish.

---

# Short Covering Can Cause Green Candles

Remember Lesson #53.

A trader closing a short may need to buy.

If many short traders close at the same time, their buying can push price upward.

So a green rally can sometimes be driven by:

## Short covering

rather than a fundamental change in long-term market demand.

---

# Short Squeezes Can Be Extremely Strong

If many traders are short and price begins rising quickly, stop losses and liquidations can force even more buying.

Price might move:

## $50 → $60 → $75

very rapidly.

But after the squeeze ends, price could still resume the broader downtrend.

A violent green move alone does not prove bearish structure has ended.

---

# Lower Highs Are Important

Suppose a market falls from:

## $100 → $70

Then rallies to:

## $90

That $90 area becomes important.

If price turns down again from there, it confirms sellers are still appearing below the old $100 high.

That is one reason traders watch:

# **Lower Highs**

so closely.

---

# Lower Lows Confirm Continued Selling Pressure

Now suppose price turns down from $90 and falls:

## $70 → $60

The market has broken the previous low.

That creates a new lower low.

Now both sides of the structure agree:

## Lower High

plus:

## Lower Low

equals:

# **Continuing bearish structure**

---

# Market Structure Matters More Than Red Candles

Imagine:

### Chart A

Lots of red candles.

But price keeps making:

- higher highs
- higher lows

### Chart B

Several green rallies.

But price keeps making:

- lower highs
- lower lows

Chart B may actually be the more bearish market.

Colour is secondary.

Structure is primary.

---

# What Is a Swing High?

A:

## **Swing High**

is a local peak where price rises, turns, and moves lower.

Example:

## $80 → $100 → $90

The:

## $100

area is a swing high.

---

# What Is a Swing Low?

A:

## **Swing Low**

is a local bottom where price falls, turns, and moves higher.

Example:

## $100 → $80 → $90

The:

## $80

area is a swing low.

Lower highs and lower lows are built from these swing points.

---

# Don't Label Every Tiny Move

Imagine price moves:

## $100 → $99.90 → $100.10 → $99.80

Those tiny movements may be meaningless noise on the timeframe you're analysing.

Focus on:

## Significant swing highs and swing lows.

Otherwise, every chart can be made to look bullish or bearish depending on how aggressively you label it.

---

# Timeframe Matters

A market can create:

## Lower highs and lower lows

on a five-minute chart while still remaining in a major weekly uptrend.

For example:

### Weekly chart

Strong uptrend.

### Daily chart

Pullback.

### 1-hour chart

Clear downtrend.

### 5-minute chart

Small bounce.

All four can exist simultaneously.

---

# Lower-Timeframe Downtrend Inside a Higher-Timeframe Uptrend

Suppose Bitcoin rises over several months:

## $60,000 → $100,000

Then during one day it falls:

## $100,000 → $94,000

The one-hour chart may show:

- lower highs
- lower lows

But the weekly structure may still be strongly bullish.

This could simply be a correction.

---

# Higher-Timeframe Structure Gives Broader Context

A downtrend on:

## 1-minute candles

may last fifteen minutes.

A downtrend on:

## weekly candles

could last months or years.

Always ask:

# **Bearish on what timeframe?**

---

# What Is Trend Continuation?

Suppose price already has bearish structure.

It forms:

## Lower High

then breaks below the previous low.

That new lower low can represent:

# **Trend Continuation**

The market is continuing its existing downward behaviour.

---

# Simple Bearish Continuation Example

High:

## $100

Low:

## $80

Lower high:

## $90

Lower low:

## $70

Lower high:

## $82

Lower low:

## $60

Every recovery is weaker.

Every decline pushes lower.

The bearish structure remains intact.

---

# What Happens When Price Stops Making Lower Lows?

This can be an early sign that conditions are changing.

Suppose:

Previous low:

## $60

Price rallies.

Then falls again but stops at:

## $65

Instead of a lower low, the market has created a:

# **Higher Low**

That does not automatically mean a new uptrend.

But bearish momentum may be weakening.

---

# What Happens When Price Breaks a Lower High?

This can be especially interesting.

Suppose the latest lower high is:

## $80

Price rallies and breaks above:

## $80 → $85

The market has broken an important piece of bearish structure.

This may suggest:

## The downtrend is weakening.

But more confirmation may still be needed.

---

# A Broken Lower High Does Not Guarantee a Bull Market

The market could:

- form a range
- fake out
- fall again
- create a slightly larger correction

One structural break tells you:

## Something changed.

It does not tell you with certainty what comes next.

---

# Break of Structure

When price breaks an important swing point, traders may call it a:

# **Break of Structure — BOS**

In a downtrend:

- breaking below the previous low can confirm bearish continuation
- breaking above an important lower high can signal bearish structure is weakening

Terminology varies between trading styles.

The underlying idea is simple:

## Watch what happens to important highs and lows.

---

# Change of Character

You may also hear:

# **CHOCH — Change of Character**

This generally describes early evidence that the previous market behaviour may be shifting.

For example:

Bearish structure:

## Lower High → Lower Low → Lower High → Lower Low

Then price suddenly:

- creates a higher low
- breaks above a previous lower high

The market's behaviour has changed.

Whether it becomes a full uptrend still needs to be seen.

---

# Keep the Concept Simple

You don't need twenty technical labels.

In a downtrend, ask:

## Is price still making lower highs?

## Is price still making lower lows?

If yes:

## Bearish structure remains.

If no:

## Pay attention — conditions may be changing.

---

# Lower Highs Can Form Resistance

Remember Lesson #58.

Suppose price rallies repeatedly but fails around:

## $100

then:

## $90

then:

## $80

Each lower high may become a new area of:

## Resistance.

This connects support and resistance directly with market structure.

---

# Lower Lows Break Support

Suppose support previously existed around:

## $70

Price falls through it to:

## $60

The lower low also represents:

## A support breakdown.

Again, several technical concepts are describing the same underlying price behaviour.

---

# Falling Trend Lines Connect Lower Highs

Remember Lesson #59.

A falling trend line is often drawn across:

# **Lower Highs**

For example:

## $100 → $90 → $80

The line helps visualise the bearish structure.

But the lower highs themselves are the market behaviour.

The line is only the drawing.

---

# Example: Trend Line + Bearish Structure

Highs:

## $100

## $90

## $82

Lows:

## $80

## $70

## $60

A falling trend line connects the highs.

The lows also keep moving lower.

The trend line and market structure agree.

---

# Strong Downtrend vs Weak Downtrend

Not every downtrend looks the same.

### Strong Downtrend

- sharp declines
- weak recoveries
- shallow rallies
- frequent new lows

### Weak Downtrend

- slower declines
- stronger rallies
- new lows only slightly below previous lows

Both may technically remain bearish.

But momentum differs.

---

# Weak Rallies Can Signal Strong Selling Pressure

Imagine:

Price falls:

## $100 → $70

Then only rebounds to:

## $75

before falling again.

Buyers could barely recover any of the decline.

That can indicate strong bearish pressure.

---

# Stronger Rallies Can Signal Weakening Momentum

Imagine:

Price falls:

## $100 → $70

Then rallies back to:

## $95

before turning lower.

The market technically still formed a lower high.

But buyers recovered much more of the previous decline.

This can suggest bearish momentum is becoming less dominant.

---

# Lower Lows Can Become Smaller

Example:

Lows:

## $80

## $60

## $50

## $47

The market is technically still creating lower lows.

But the size of each new decline is shrinking.

Momentum may be weakening.

That does not guarantee reversal.

It simply means the character of the decline is changing.

---

# Price Can Consolidate During a Downtrend

Markets do not always fall continuously.

Suppose:

## $100 → $60

Then price spends several weeks between:

## $55 and $65

The market is now:

# **Consolidating**

It may eventually:

- break lower and continue the downtrend
- break higher and begin changing structure

Until then, direction is less clear.

---

# Downtrend → Range → Uptrend

Markets often transition gradually.

Example:

### Stage 1

Lower highs and lower lows.

### Stage 2

Price stops making lower lows.

### Stage 3

Market trades sideways.

### Stage 4

Higher low forms.

### Stage 5

Previous resistance breaks.

### Stage 6

Higher high forms.

The market may transition from:

## Downtrend

to:

## Range

to:

## Uptrend.

---

# A Reversal Is Usually a Process

Beginners often look for one magical candle that marks the exact bottom.

Real trend reversals may take time.

The market can gradually stop:

## Making lower lows.

Then start:

## Making higher lows.

Then break:

## Lower highs.

Then create:

## Higher highs.

That structural transition is often more useful than trying to guess the exact bottom.

---

# Trying to Catch the Bottom Is Dangerous

Suppose a token falls:

## $100 → $80

You think:

> “Cheap now.”

You buy.

It falls:

## $80 → $60

You think:

> “Definitely bottom.”

You buy more.

Then:

## $60 → $40

The market kept producing lower lows.

Trying to catch every falling price can be dangerous.

---

# “It's Down 80%, It Can't Fall Much More”

This is a classic mistake.

Suppose a token falls:

## $100 → $20

That is an:

## 80% decline.

If it then falls:

## $20 → $10

that is another:

## 50% loss

from your new entry.

A large historical decline does not protect a new buyer from further losses.

---

# A 90% Drop Can Be Followed by Another 90% Drop

Example:

Token begins:

## $100

Falls 90%:

## $10

Falls another 90%:

## $1

The second 90% decline is calculated from the new $10 price.

This is why:

> “It has already fallen so much”

is not a complete trading thesis.

---

# Lower Highs Can Help Avoid FOMO During Bounces

Imagine price has fallen heavily.

Then it rallies:

## +20%

The move feels huge.

But if it still stops below the previous swing high, it may simply be another lower high.

Looking at structure can help separate:

## Strong-looking bounce

from:

## Confirmed trend reversal.

---

# Example

Previous high:

## $100

Market falls:

## $50

Then rallies:

## $50 → $75

That is a:

## 50% rally

from $50.

Impressive.

But:

## $75 < $100

So on the larger structure, it may still only be a:

# Lower High.

---

# Percentage Moves Can Be Misleading During Declines

After large declines, percentage rallies can look enormous.

Example:

## $100 → $20

Then:

## $20 → $30

That is a:

## 50% rally.

But the asset is still:

## 70% below

its original $100 level.

Always consider the broader price structure.

---

# Volume Adds Context

Suppose price creates a new lower low on:

## very high volume.

That may suggest strong market participation in the sell-off.

If the next bounce occurs on:

## very low volume,

it may be less convincing.

This does not guarantee continuation.

It simply adds information.

---

# Capitulation

Sometimes a downtrend ends with an extreme wave of selling known as:

# **Capitulation**

This may involve:

- very large red candles
- extreme volume
- forced liquidations
- panic selling

Afterwards, the market may bounce strongly.

But identifying capitulation perfectly in real time is difficult.

Do not assume every large red candle marks the bottom.

---

# Lower Lows Can Be Caused by Liquidations

In leveraged markets, falling prices can liquidate long positions.

Those forced sales push price lower.

That can liquidate additional longs.

The result may be:

## rapid lower lows

during a:

# **Liquidation Cascade**

This can temporarily accelerate bearish structure.

---

# DEX Liquidity Can Exaggerate Lower Lows

Low-liquidity token pools can fall dramatically from relatively small sales.

Imagine a shallow token pool.

One large holder sells.

Price falls:

## $1 → $0.60

That huge lower low may represent one large transaction rather than broad market consensus.

Liquidity matters.

---

# Low-Liquidity Bounces Can Be Misleading Too

The same market may then bounce:

## $0.60 → $0.80

on a small buy.

The chart looks volatile.

But both the decline and recovery may be driven by very little capital.

This is why structure should always be considered alongside:

- liquidity
- volume

---

# Different Markets Can Show Different Structure

A token may trade across:

- centralised exchanges
- DEX pools
- different quote assets

One venue may briefly create a lower low that another market does not.

Arbitrage may later bring prices closer together.

Always know:

## Which market you're analysing.

---

# Bearish Structure Does Not Mean the Project Is Bad

A legitimate project can experience a downtrend.

Markets move because of:

- broader sentiment
- macro conditions
- profit taking
- liquidity
- speculation

Likewise, a bad project can temporarily have bullish chart structure.

Technical structure tells you about:

## Price behaviour

not project quality.

---

# Lower Highs and Lower Lows Do Not Mean “Short Everything”

Identifying a downtrend is not the same as receiving a trading signal.

A short position has its own risks.

Remember:

- short squeezes
- unlimited theoretical upside
- leverage
- liquidation
- funding

You still need a complete risk plan.

---

# Don't Short After an Already Massive Collapse Without Thinking

Imagine a token drops:

## $100 → $20

and you open a huge leveraged short because:

> “Downtrend.”

Then price experiences a violent relief rally:

## $20 → $35

The larger trend may still be bearish.

But the short can still lose heavily or be liquidated.

Direction alone is not enough.

Entry and risk matter.

---

# Bearish Structure Can Help Define Risk

Suppose a trader shorts near a lower high.

Their thesis might be:

> “I expect the bearish structure to continue.”

If price breaks strongly above the previous important lower high, that thesis may be weakening.

This can help define:

## Invalidation.

---

# Example

Previous high:

## $100

Lower high:

## $80

Trader shorts near:

## $78

If price breaks:

## $80 → $90

the bearish structure may no longer support the original idea.

The trader can define risk around that structure rather than simply holding indefinitely.

---

# Don't Keep Moving the Bearish Invalidation

Suppose the key lower high is:

## $80

Price breaks to:

## $85

The trader says:

> “Actually $90 is the real level.”

Then price hits:

## $95

> “Maybe $100.”

This is the same emotional mistake we discussed with bullish trades.

If the structure changes:

## Update the analysis.

Don't endlessly rewrite the trade thesis to avoid accepting a loss.

---

# Failed Breakdown

Suppose support is:

## $50

Price drops to:

## $47

creating what looks like a lower low.

Then price quickly recovers:

## $47 → $60

The breakdown failed.

A brief wick below the previous low does not always confirm sustained bearish continuation.

---

# Candle Closes Matter

Some traders look for:

## A close below the previous low

rather than only a wick.

Example:

Previous low:

## $50

Price wicks:

## $48

but closes:

## $53

That may be interpreted differently from a candle that:

## closes at $45.

Again, there is no universal rule.

But candle closes can add useful context.

---

# Bear Trap

A:

# **Bear Trap**

can occur when price appears to break down, attracting sellers and shorts, before rapidly reversing upward.

Example:

Support:

## $50

Breakdown:

## $48

Traders short.

Price suddenly rallies:

## $48 → $60

Those new shorts are trapped.

Their exits can add even more buying pressure.

---

# Bear Traps Can End Downtrends — But Not Always

A failed lower low can sometimes be the beginning of a structural reversal.

But it could also just be temporary volatility.

Watch what happens next.

Does price:

- create a higher low?
- break a lower high?
- establish a higher high?

The sequence matters.

---

# How a Downtrend Can Begin to Reverse

Imagine:

### Previous high

## $100

### Lower high

## $80

### Lower low

## $50

Then:

### New low

## $55

This is not a lower low.

It is a:

## Higher Low.

Then price rallies above:

## $80

Now the previous lower high has been broken.

If price later forms another higher low and higher high:

## Bullish structure may be emerging.

---

# Structure Shift Example

Bearish sequence:

## High $100

## Low $70

## High $90

## Low $60

Then:

## High $80

## Low $65

Notice:

## $65 > $60

First higher low.

Then price rallies:

## $80 → $95

Now:

## $95 > $80

The market created a higher high relative to the recent structure.

The bearish pattern is no longer behaving the same way.

---

# Not Every Downtrend Ends With a V-Shaped Recovery

Sometimes price crashes:

## $100 → $50

then instantly rallies:

## $50 → $100

But many reversals are slower.

Price may spend months forming:

- a base
- range
- higher lows
- resistance tests

before a clear new uptrend develops.

---

# “No Clear Trend” Is a Valid Answer

If price stops making lower lows but hasn't begun making higher highs either, the market may simply be:

# **Range-Bound**

You do not have to force:

## Bullish

or:

## Bearish

onto every chart.

Sometimes:

## Neutral

is the most accurate description.

---

# Common Beginner Lower-High / Lower-Low Mistakes

## 1. Thinking Every Green Candle Ends the Downtrend

Bearish markets contain rallies.

---

## 2. Labeling Every Tiny Move

Focus on significant swings.

---

## 3. Ignoring Timeframe

Short-term bearish structure can exist inside a long-term uptrend.

---

## 4. Buying Because Something “Already Fell a Lot”

Price can continue falling.

---

## 5. Treating One Lower Low as Proof

Watch both highs and lows.

---

## 6. Ignoring Broken Lower Highs

If important resistance begins failing, bearish structure may be changing.

---

## 7. Shorting Every Lower Low

Entry timing and risk still matter.

---

## 8. Ignoring Short-Squeeze Risk

Downtrends can contain violent rallies.

---

## 9. Ignoring Liquidity

Thin markets create misleading structures.

---

## 10. Refusing to Update the Analysis

If structure changes, your view should be allowed to change too.

# A Better Beginner Routine

When looking for a downtrend, ask:

## 1. Where is the previous significant swing high?

## 2. Did the rally create a lower high?

## 3. Where is the previous swing low?

## 4. Did price create a lower low?

## 5. What timeframe am I analysing?

## 6. Is volume supporting the decline?

## 7. Is liquidity deep or thin?

## 8. Did price close below support or only wick through it?

## 9. Is bearish momentum strengthening or weakening?

## 10. What would invalidate the bearish structure?

This gives you a much clearer picture than:

> “There are lots of red candles.”

# The Key Lesson

Lower highs and lower lows are one of the simplest ways to identify a downtrend.

Remember:

### 📉 LOWER HIGH

**A rally that fails below the previous important high.**

### ⬇️ LOWER LOW

**A decline that pushes below the previous important low.**

### 🟢 RELIEF RALLY

**An upward move that can occur inside a larger downtrend.**

### 💥 STRUCTURE BREAK

**Price breaks an important swing point and signals the previous pattern may be changing.**

Most importantly:

# **Downtrends are not made only from red candles. They are made from structure.**

A bearish market can contain:

- large green candles
- violent rallies
- short squeezes
- temporary recoveries

What matters is whether the market continues to create:

# **Lower Highs and Lower Lows**

When that structure begins to fail, pay attention.

The market may be transitioning into something new.
