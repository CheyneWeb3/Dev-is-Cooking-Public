# What Is Trading Volume?

Trading volume measures the amount of an asset that was traded during a particular period.

For example, if:

## 1,000 ETH

changed hands during one hour, the trading volume for that period could be described as:

## 1,000 ETH

Depending on the platform, volume may also be displayed in the value of the quote currency.

For example:

## $3,000,000 of ETH traded

during the same period.

---

# Volume Measures Activity

The simplest way to think about volume is:

# **Volume = Trading Activity**

Higher volume means:

## More trading occurred.

Lower volume means:

## Less trading occurred.

But volume alone does not automatically tell you whether the market is bullish or bearish.

You need to look at:

# **Price + Volume**

together.

---

# Simple Volume Example

Imagine Token A trades at:

## $10

During one hour:

## 100,000 tokens

are traded.

That represents approximately:

## $1,000,000

of trading activity if all trades occurred around $10.

Now imagine Token B is also:

## $10

but only:

## 1,000 tokens

trade during the same hour.

That's approximately:

## $10,000

of trading activity.

Both tokens have the same displayed price.

Their markets are completely different.

---

# Price Does Not Tell You Volume

This is important.

A token priced at:

## $1,000

can have very little trading activity.

Another token priced at:

## $0.01

could trade billions of units every day.

The price of one token tells you nothing by itself about:

- liquidity
- trading activity
- market depth
- market size

Volume is a separate measurement.

---

# Where Do You See Volume?

On most candlestick charts, volume is shown underneath the main price chart.

You may see vertical bars like:

## ▂ ▅ █ ▃ ▇

Each bar usually corresponds to the same timeframe as the candle above it.

For example:

If you're looking at:

## 1-hour candles

each volume bar normally represents:

## the volume traded during that hour.

---

# Candle Timeframe and Volume Timeframe Match

Suppose you're using:

## 5-minute candles.

Each volume bar represents approximately:

## 5 minutes of trading activity.

Switch to:

## Daily candles.

Now each volume bar represents:

## an entire day.

This is why volume should always be interpreted alongside the chart timeframe.

---

# What Does a Tall Volume Bar Mean?

A tall volume bar generally means:

## A lot of trading occurred during that period.

For example:

Average hourly volume:

## $1 million

Then one hour suddenly records:

## $20 million

That period had unusually high participation.

Something may have caused traders to become much more active.

---

# What Does a Small Volume Bar Mean?

A small volume bar means:

## Relatively little trading occurred.

For example:

Average hourly volume:

## $1 million

Current hour:

## $50,000

The market is comparatively quiet.

There may be fewer buyers and sellers actively trading.

---

# Volume Bars Can Be Green or Red Too

Many charting platforms colour volume bars based on the candle associated with them.

For example:

## Green volume bar

may correspond to a candle that closed above its open.

## Red volume bar

may correspond to a candle that closed below its open.

But this does not literally mean:

> “All of this volume was buying.”

or:

> “All of this volume was selling.”

Every completed trade has:

## a buyer

and:

## a seller.

---

# Every Trade Has Two Sides

This is one of the most misunderstood concepts in trading.

If someone buys:

## 1 BTC

someone else sold:

## 1 BTC.

So if the exchange reports:

## 1 BTC of trading volume

it does not mean:

## 1 BTC bought plus 1 BTC sold = 2 BTC volume.

The completed trade itself represents the volume.

Buyer and seller are two sides of the same transaction.

---

# So What Does “Buying Volume” Mean?

When traders talk casually about:

## Buying volume

they usually mean trading activity occurring alongside:

## rising prices

or aggressive buyers taking available sell orders.

Likewise:

## Selling volume

often refers to high activity while price is falling.

But every trade still contains both:

# A buyer and a seller.

The difference is which side is being more aggressive.

---

# Aggressive Buyers

Imagine sellers are offering tokens at:

## $10.00

## $10.05

## $10.10

Buyers aggressively use market orders and keep purchasing the available sell orders.

Price moves higher.

We might describe this as:

## Strong buying pressure.

The trades still had sellers.

But buyers were willing to keep paying progressively higher prices.

---

# Aggressive Sellers

Now imagine buyers are bidding:

## $10.00

## $9.95

## $9.90

Sellers aggressively sell into those bids.

Price moves lower.

We might describe this as:

## Strong selling pressure.

Again, every transaction still had a buyer.

The sellers were simply the more aggressive side.

---

# Price + Volume Gives More Context

Imagine two breakouts above:

## $100 resistance.

### Breakout A

Price:

## $100 → $105

Volume:

## Very low.

### Breakout B

Price:

## $100 → $105

Volume:

## Five times normal.

Both broke resistance.

But Breakout B had much more trading participation behind it.

That difference may matter.

---

# High Volume Can Confirm Interest

When price makes an important move alongside unusually high volume, it can indicate:

## More market participants were involved.

For example:

- breakout above resistance
- breakdown below support
- large reversal
- sudden news reaction

High volume does not guarantee the move will continue.

But it tells you:

# **The market was active.**

---

# Low-Volume Breakouts Can Be Less Convincing

Imagine resistance is:

## $10

Price barely reaches:

## $10.10

on very low volume.

There may not be much participation supporting the breakout.

Price could easily fall back below resistance.

This is sometimes called:

## A weak breakout.

But remember:

Low volume does not automatically mean failure.

It simply gives you another piece of information.

---

# Volume Does Not Predict the Future

A huge volume spike does not automatically mean:

## Buy.

It also does not automatically mean:

## Sell.

Volume tells you:

## Something caused significant trading activity.

You still need to understand:

- where price is
- market structure
- support and resistance
- trend
- liquidity
- news or events

---

# High Volume During a Price Rise

Suppose a token moves:

## $10 → $15

while volume rises dramatically.

This may indicate:

## strong participation in the upward move.

Buyers are aggressively competing for available supply.

That can make the move more significant than a similar price rise occurring on very little volume.

---

# High Volume During a Price Fall

Now imagine:

## $10 → $5

with huge volume.

That indicates intense trading during the decline.

Possible reasons include:

- panic selling
- liquidations
- major holders exiting
- bad news
- stop-loss cascades

Again:

## High volume describes activity.

It does not automatically tell you what comes next.

---

# Rising Price + Rising Volume

A common interpretation is:

## Price rising

plus:

## Volume rising

can show increasing participation in the upward move.

For example:

Price:

## $50 → $60 → $70

Volume:

## 1M → 2M → 4M

More trading activity is entering as price climbs.

Some traders consider this a healthier trend than price rising while activity disappears.

---

# Rising Price + Falling Volume

Now imagine:

Price:

## $50 → $60 → $70

but volume falls:

## 5M → 2M → 500K

Price is still rising.

But fewer trades are participating.

This may suggest:

## momentum is weakening.

It does not guarantee reversal.

But the move may deserve closer attention.

---

# Falling Price + Rising Volume

Suppose:

Price:

## $100 → $90 → $80

Volume:

## 1M → 3M → 8M

Selling activity is increasing as price declines.

This can indicate strong bearish participation.

---

# Falling Price + Falling Volume

Now imagine:

Price:

## $100 → $95 → $90

Volume:

## 10M → 4M → 1M

The market continues falling, but trading activity is shrinking.

This may suggest selling pressure is becoming less active.

Again:

## Not a guaranteed reversal.

Just additional context.

---

# Volume Spikes

A:

# **Volume Spike**

occurs when trading activity suddenly becomes much higher than normal.

Example:

Typical hourly volume:

## $500,000

Suddenly:

## $10 million

trades during one hour.

That is a major change.

---

# What Can Cause Volume Spikes?

Possible causes include:

- breaking news
- exchange listings
- token launches
- major announcements
- whale transactions
- liquidations
- market crashes
- breakouts
- panic
- hype

The volume tells you something significant happened in trading activity.

It does not tell you why.

---

# Volume at Support

Imagine price falls toward:

## $50 support.

For several days, volume is low.

Then at $50:

## volume explodes

and price sharply rebounds.

That may indicate substantial trading interest around the support zone.

It can make the reaction more noteworthy.

---

# Volume at Resistance

Likewise:

Price reaches:

## $100 resistance

and suddenly very high volume appears.

If price is repeatedly rejected, heavy selling may be occurring.

If price breaks through strongly, aggressive buying may be absorbing the available sell orders.

You need to look at:

## Price reaction + volume.

---

# Volume During a Breakout

Suppose resistance is:

## $100

Price breaks:

## $100 → $110

while volume triples.

That tells you:

## The breakout attracted significant trading activity.

Many traders use increased volume as one form of breakout confirmation.

---

# Volume During a Fake Breakout

Now imagine:

Resistance:

## $100

Price briefly spikes to:

## $103

on low volume.

Then collapses to:

## $90.

The low-volume move above resistance may have lacked strong participation.

This could become a:

## False breakout.

---

# High Volume Can Still Accompany a Failed Breakout

Don't turn this into:

> “High volume breakout = guaranteed success.”

Suppose price breaks resistance with enormous volume.

Then even larger selling appears.

Price falls back below the level.

High volume means:

## Significant participation.

It does not tell you which side will ultimately win.

---

# Volume During Trend Continuation

Imagine an uptrend produces:

- higher highs
- higher lows

and rallies occur with:

## strong volume

while pullbacks occur with:

## weaker volume.

Some traders consider this supportive of the bullish trend.

Why?

Because stronger participation appears when price moves with the trend.

---

# Volume During Pullbacks

A pullback on lower volume can sometimes suggest:

## Less aggressive selling.

For example:

Rally:

## $50 → $70

Volume:

## $20 million

Pullback:

## $70 → $65

Volume:

## $2 million

Then another rally begins.

The smaller pullback volume may indicate less participation against the trend.

But it still isn't a guarantee.

---

# Volume During Downtrends

The opposite can occur.

Strong down moves:

## high volume.

Small recoveries:

## low volume.

This can suggest sellers remain dominant.

Again, look at structure as well.

---

# What Is Average Volume?

One volume bar by itself is difficult to judge.

You need something to compare it with.

If today's volume is:

## $5 million

is that high?

Maybe.

If average daily volume is:

## $500,000

then yes.

If average daily volume is:

## $500 million

then $5 million is extremely low.

Volume is relative.

---

# Compare Volume With Recent History

A simple beginner approach is:

## Look at the previous volume bars.

Ask:

> “Is current volume unusually high or unusually low compared with recent periods?”

This often provides enough context without needing complicated indicators.

---

# Volume Moving Averages

Some charts add a moving average over the volume bars.

For example:

## 20-period average volume.

This can help show whether current trading activity is above or below the recent average.

We'll cover moving averages separately later.

---

# Volume vs Liquidity

This is extremely important.

# **Volume and liquidity are not the same thing.**

Volume means:

## How much trading occurred over a period.

Liquidity means:

## How much trading can occur around the current price without causing excessive price movement.

A market can have high volume but temporarily poor liquidity.

A market can also have deep liquidity but relatively quiet volume during a particular period.

---

# Example

Market A:

## Daily volume: $100 million

but available liquidity near the current price may vary throughout the day.

Market B:

## Daily volume: $5 million

but might still have relatively stable order-book depth for smaller trades.

The two concepts are connected.

But they are not interchangeable.

---

# Volume vs Market Cap

These are also completely different.

Market cap is approximately:

## Price × Circulating Supply

Trading volume measures:

## Trading activity during a period.

A token could have:

## $1 billion market cap

and only:

## $1 million daily volume.

Another could have:

## $100 million market cap

and:

## $500 million daily volume.

Market cap and volume answer different questions.

---

# Volume Can Be Larger Than Market Cap

Beginners sometimes think:

> “How can daily volume be larger than the token's market cap?”

Because the same tokens can trade multiple times during the day.

Example:

A token has:

## $100 million market cap.

The same pool of tokens may change hands repeatedly.

Daily trading volume could reach:

## $300 million

without requiring $300 million of unique new capital.

Volume measures transactions.

Not unique money deposited.

---

# Volume Is Not Net Money Flow

Suppose:

## $10 million

of a token trades today.

That does not mean:

## $10 million entered the token.

Remember:

Every trade has:

- buyer
- seller

The asset changed hands.

Volume measures turnover.

It is not the same as net capital inflow.

---

# Example

Alice buys:

## $10,000

of a token from Bob.

Volume:

## $10,000

Bob later buys:

## $10,000

back from Alice.

Additional volume:

## $10,000

Total volume:

## $20,000

But that does not mean $20,000 of new money permanently entered the market.

The same capital and assets can circulate repeatedly.

---

# Huge Volume Does Not Equal Huge New Investment

This is particularly important during hype cycles.

A token might report:

## $500 million daily volume.

That does not mean:

## Investors added $500 million of new capital that day.

It means approximately that amount of trading activity occurred.

Very different concept.

---

# DEX Trading Volume

On a decentralised exchange, volume usually measures swaps executed through:

## A liquidity pool

or group of pools.

For example:

TOKEN/USDC pool:

## $5 million 24-hour volume.

That means swaps worth approximately $5 million occurred through that pool during the measured period.

---

# DEX Volume Comes From Swaps

Imagine a liquidity pool contains:

## TOKEN + USDC

Traders repeatedly swap:

- TOKEN → USDC
- USDC → TOKEN

Those swaps contribute to trading volume.

Liquidity providers may earn fees based partly on this activity.

---

# DEX Volume vs DEX Liquidity

Suppose a pool has:

## $1 million liquidity

and:

## $10 million daily volume.

That means the pool's liquidity effectively turned over many times during the day.

Another pool might have:

## $10 million liquidity

but only:

## $100,000 volume.

Very different usage patterns.

---

# Volume-to-Liquidity Ratio

Traders and liquidity providers sometimes compare:

## Trading Volume

with:

## Available Liquidity.

A pool generating large trading volume relative to its liquidity may be highly active.

But very high volume can also accompany:

- volatility
- arbitrage
- rapid price changes

So context matters.

---

# Arbitrage Creates Volume

Suppose Token X trades at:

## $1.00 on DEX A

and:

## $1.10 on DEX B.

Arbitrage traders may:

- buy on A
- sell on B

These trades create volume on both markets.

The activity helps bring prices closer together.

So high DEX volume does not always represent people simply investing in or exiting a token.

Some of it may be:

## Arbitrage activity.

---

# Market Makers Create Volume Too

Professional market makers continuously buy and sell assets.

Their activity can generate significant volume while helping markets maintain:

- tighter spreads
- deeper liquidity

Again:

## Volume does not automatically equal retail investor demand.

---

# Bots Can Generate Volume

Crypto markets contain substantial automated trading.

Bots can:

- arbitrage
- market make
- rebalance
- execute strategies
- trade between venues

These transactions all contribute to reported volume.

A high-volume market may therefore include significant automated activity.

---

# Wash Trading

Unfortunately, some markets may also contain:

# **Wash Trading**

This involves creating artificial-looking trading activity, often by trading between related accounts or otherwise manufacturing volume.

The purpose can be to make a market appear:

## More active than it really is.

This is one reason reported volume should not always be accepted blindly.

---

# Not All Reported Volume Is Equal

When comparing platforms, consider:

- exchange reputation
- liquidity
- spreads
- order-book depth
- on-chain data
- actual trade execution

A claimed:

## $1 billion volume

means less if the underlying market appears extremely thin or manipulated.

---

# On-Chain Volume Can Be Verified More Directly

DEX activity is recorded on-chain.

This allows analysts to inspect:

- swaps
- transaction amounts
- pool activity

However, even on-chain volume can include:

- arbitrage
- bots
- related wallets
- repeated trading

Blockchain visibility makes activity easier to inspect.

It does not automatically tell you the motivation behind every transaction.

---

# A Huge Candle Does Not Mean Huge Volume

This is a critical lesson.

Imagine a tiny token pool has:

## $10,000 liquidity.

Someone buys:

## $2,000.

That could move price dramatically.

The chart might show:

## +30%

with one giant green candle.

But trading volume may still be tiny compared with major markets.

---

# Small Volume Can Create Huge Price Moves

This happens because:

# **Price movement depends on available liquidity.**

In a shallow market, there may not be many tokens available near the current price.

A relatively small market order consumes that liquidity and pushes price quickly.

---

# Large Volume Can Create Small Price Moves

Now imagine Bitcoin.

Billions of dollars may trade while price only moves:

## 1%.

Why?

Because the market has much deeper liquidity.

Large amounts of buying and selling can occur without dramatically changing the price.

---

# Candle Size and Volume Are Different Measurements

Remember:

### Candle Size

Shows:

## Price movement.

### Volume Bar

Shows:

## Trading activity.

You need both to understand what happened.

---

# Example A — Huge Candle, Tiny Volume

Token rises:

## 50%

Volume:

## $20,000

Possible explanation:

## Very low liquidity.

---

# Example B — Small Candle, Huge Volume

Bitcoin moves:

## 1%

Volume:

## $5 billion

Massive trading activity occurred.

But buyers and sellers remained relatively balanced, limiting the net price movement.

---

# High Volume With Little Price Movement

This can be interesting.

Suppose enormous volume occurs around:

## $100

but price remains around:

## $99–$101.

This suggests large amounts of buying and selling are being absorbed without price moving very far.

Traders sometimes call this:

## Absorption

depending on the exact market behaviour.

---

# Buyers and Sellers Can Battle at One Level

Imagine resistance at:

## $100.

Buyers repeatedly purchase available sell orders.

Sellers keep supplying more tokens.

Volume becomes enormous.

Yet price barely moves.

This tells you:

## A lot of trading is happening around that level.

Eventually one side may run out of pressure.

---

# Volume and Breakouts From Consolidation

Imagine a token trades between:

## $90 and $100

for several weeks.

Volume becomes increasingly quiet.

Then suddenly:

## price breaks above $100

and volume surges.

That combination can indicate significant new market participation.

Some traders consider that more meaningful than a breakout occurring during extremely quiet activity.

---

# Volume Can Increase Near Major News

If a project announces something significant, traders may react immediately.

Volume can explode as participants:

- buy
- sell
- reposition
- hedge

The direction of price tells you the net result.

Volume tells you:

## how much trading activity occurred during the reaction.

---

# Volume Can Spike During Panic

During a market crash, volume often rises dramatically because:

- investors sell
- stop losses trigger
- leveraged positions liquidate
- short sellers enter
- bargain buyers buy

A high-volume crash contains many different types of participants.

---

# Capitulation Volume

A major sell-off may sometimes end with:

## extremely high volume.

Traders may describe this as:

# **Capitulation**

The idea is that large numbers of holders finally sell after a prolonged decline.

But identifying true capitulation in real time is difficult.

A volume spike does not guarantee the bottom.

---

# Exhaustion Volume

You may also hear traders describe:

# **Exhaustion**

after an extended move.

Example:

Price rises dramatically.

Volume becomes enormous.

Then price struggles to continue higher.

Some traders interpret this as buyers becoming exhausted.

But volume alone cannot prove that.

The subsequent price action matters.

---

# Volume Divergence

A:

## **Volume Divergence**

can describe situations where price and volume appear to be moving in different directions.

For example:

Price keeps making:

## Higher highs

while volume keeps falling.

Some traders see that as a sign the trend has less participation behind it.

Again:

## Not a guaranteed reversal.

---

# Falling Volume Is Normal During Quiet Markets

Not every reduction in volume is meaningful.

Markets naturally have quieter periods.

Volume can vary based on:

- time of day
- day of week
- market conditions
- news
- volatility

You should compare activity with an appropriate recent baseline.

---

# Crypto Trades 24/7

Unlike many traditional markets, crypto trades:

## 24 hours a day

## 7 days a week.

Volume may still vary substantially depending on which global markets are active.

Some periods are naturally busier than others.

---

# Daily Volume Depends on the Platform

A token's volume on:

## Exchange A

may be very different from:

## Exchange B.

Likewise, one DEX pool may show:

## $1 million

while another shows:

## $10 million.

Always check whether the number represents:

- one market
- one exchange
- one pool
- all tracked venues

---

# 24-Hour Volume Is a Rolling Metric

Many crypto platforms show:

## 24h Volume.

This often means trading activity during the:

## previous rolling 24 hours

rather than one fixed calendar day.

As old trades leave the 24-hour window and new ones enter, the number changes.

---

# Volume Can Drop Without Anyone “Removing Money”

Suppose yesterday's 24-hour volume was:

## $100 million.

Today it falls to:

## $20 million.

That does not mean:

## $80 million was withdrawn.

It simply means less trading activity occurred during the current measurement window.

This distinction is crucial.

---

# Volume Is Flow — Not Stored Value

A useful way to remember it:

# **Market Cap = valuation snapshot**

# **Liquidity = available trading depth**

# **Volume = trading activity over time**

These three concepts are related.

But they measure different things.

---

# Example

Token X:

## Market Cap: $100 million

## Liquidity: $5 million

## 24h Volume: $20 million

This does not mean:

## $125 million exists somewhere in one pile.

Each number measures something different.

---

# Volume Alone Cannot Tell You Whether to Buy

Imagine a token suddenly gets:

## 10× normal volume.

Possible explanations include:

- huge buying
- huge selling
- a market battle
- arbitrage
- exchange listing
- panic
- bot activity

You need the chart and context.

---

# Volume Alone Cannot Tell You Whether to Sell

High volume at a market bottom can sometimes occur before recovery.

High volume at a market top can sometimes occur before decline.

High volume can also occur halfway through a trend.

Volume tells you:

# **Pay attention. Something is happening.**

It does not tell you exactly what action to take.

# Common Beginner Volume Mistakes

## 1. Thinking Volume Means Money Entering the Market

Every trade has a buyer and seller.

Volume measures trading activity.

---

## 2. Confusing Volume With Liquidity

A high-volume market is not automatically deeply liquid at every moment.

---

## 3. Confusing Volume With Market Cap

They measure completely different things.

---

## 4. Assuming High Volume Is Bullish

High volume can accompany massive selling.

---

## 5. Assuming Low Volume Is Bearish

Quiet markets can still rise.

---

## 6. Looking at Volume Without Comparing Recent History

$1 million may be huge for one token and tiny for another.

---

## 7. Ignoring the Timeframe

A one-minute volume spike is different from record weekly volume.

---

## 8. Assuming a Huge Candle Means Huge Volume

Low liquidity can create massive price movement from small trades.

---

## 9. Trusting Every Reported Volume Number

Some markets can contain artificial or low-quality activity.

---

## 10. Using Volume as a Standalone Trading Signal

Volume works best as context alongside price and market structure.

# A Better Beginner Volume Routine

When looking at a chart, ask:

## 1. Is current volume high or low compared with recent periods?

## 2. Is price rising or falling?

## 3. Is volume increasing or decreasing with the trend?

## 4. Is price breaking an important support or resistance level?

## 5. Did volume increase during the breakout?

## 6. Is the market highly liquid or thin?

## 7. Is this one exchange or the entire market?

## 8. Could arbitrage or bots be contributing?

## 9. Did a news event cause the spike?

## 10. What is market structure doing at the same time?

Now volume becomes useful information rather than just another coloured bar.

# The Key Lesson

Trading volume tells you:

# **How much trading activity occurred during a period.**

Remember:

### 📊 HIGH VOLUME

**A large amount of trading occurred.**

### 💤 LOW VOLUME

**Relatively little trading occurred.**

### 💥 VOLUME SPIKE

**Trading activity increased sharply compared with normal levels.**

### 🕯️ PRICE CANDLE

**Shows how far price moved.**

### 💧 LIQUIDITY

**Shows how easily the market can absorb trades without major price movement.**

And most importantly:

# **Volume is not money entering the market.**

Every completed trade contains:

## a buyer

and:

## a seller.

Volume measures how much trading occurred between them.

Use volume to understand the strength and participation behind a price move.

Don't use it as a prediction by itself.
